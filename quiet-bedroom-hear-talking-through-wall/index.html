How to Make a Bedroom Quiet Enough to Sleep When You Can Hear Talking Through the Wall

It is midnight and you can follow your neighbor’s phone call word for word. Not loud, exactly. Just clear enough that your brain will not stop listening.

The short version: When you can hear talking through a bedroom wall, start with free moves: put the bed on a different wall and keep the headboard off the shared one. Then add mass on that wall, like a full bookcase or heavy padded blankets on a freestanding rack. Use masking with more low end, like brown noise, placed between your head and the wall. Earplugs or sleep headphones handle what is left. If the talking happens during quiet hours, raise it politely, then in writing.

A post on r/soundproof asked how to soundproof a bedroom wall to get some sleep, describing being able to hear talking, belching, and snoring through it. Voices are hard because your brain is wired to pick out speech. Even quiet talking can wake you, while louder steady noise does not. This guide is about breaking that up, not about turning a rental wall into a studio.

Why you can hear every word

Interior walls in many apartments are thin: a layer of drywall on each side of a stud, sometimes with little or no insulation. Speech sits in the middle of the frequency range, where those walls do a poor job. Sound also leaks through gaps: outlets back to back, gaps under the baseboard, and shared ducts. Renters can not change the wall itself. What you can change is how close you are to it, how much mass and softness sits in front of it, and what else your ears hear.

Move the bed first

If your headboard is on the shared wall, your head is a few inches from the noise. Moving the bed to another wall, or even turning it, can make more difference than anything you buy. If you cannot move it, pull the bed a few inches off the wall and put something soft and heavy in the gap, like a padded headboard or pillows.

Also figure out which wall the voices actually come through. Stand at each wall at night and listen. Sometimes the talking comes through a closet that backs onto the neighbor’s living room, or through the door from a shared hallway, not the wall you assumed. A closet full of clothes is a good buffer, so keep it full and the door closed. If the sound is coming in at the door, our guide to light and noise leaks around the bedroom door covers sweeps and seals.

Add mass to the shared wall

Heavy things in front of the wall add some mass and break up the sound path. They will not make the wall soundproof, but they can take the edge off.

  • A full bookcase. A wooden 5-shelf bookcase or a NUMENN 5-tier bookshelf filled with books is one of the best renter options. Ask before anchoring tall furniture to the wall. In many buildings, anchors for tip-over safety are allowed or expected.
  • Padded blankets. Heavy moving blankets hung from a sturdy garment rack in front of the wall, or draped over a bookcase, add soft mass. They are not pretty, so many people use them behind a headboard or a curtain.
  • A wardrobe or dresser. Any big, heavy piece of furniture along the shared wall helps a bit.

Foam panels are mostly for echo inside a room. They do little to block a neighbor’s voice, and the adhesive is a risk on rental paint.

Check the outlets on the shared wall. If your outlet lines up with your neighbor’s, sound can pass through the box. Foam outlet gaskets behind the cover plate are cheap and come out easily, but turn the power off before removing a cover plate.

Use masking that covers voices

Plain white noise can sound hissy and still let voices through. Many people find brown or pink noise, which has more low-frequency sound, covers speech better. A LectroFan EVO lets you try fan sounds, white, pink, and brown noise. A Yogasleep Dohm has a real fan inside if you prefer a natural sound. Put the machine between your head and the shared wall, not across the room. Keep it at a volume you can sleep through every night.

Then handle what is left at your ears

  • Foam earplugs. Mack’s Ultra Soft are cheap and strong if you fit them well.
  • Reusable earplugs. Loop Quiet 2 are soft silicone. Some people find them more comfortable for side sleeping. Check the listing for the noise rating.
  • Sleep headphones. A headband with flat speakers can play brown noise right at your ears.

Bring it up with the neighbor or the landlord

Late-night talking during quiet hours is a reasonable thing to raise. Many people do not know how far their voice carries. A friendly note can work: say which wall, what time, and that you can hear conversations clearly. If it continues, keep a short log and contact management in writing, quoting the quiet-hours part of your lease. You can also ask management whether gaps on the shared wall, like loose outlets or open baseboards, can be sealed. That is a reasonable maintenance request. What not to do

  • Do not glue foam or egg-crate panels to the wall. They do little for voices, and the adhesive can damage paint.
  • Do not pound on the wall. It escalates and rarely helps.
  • Do not block a heater, vent, or window with blankets or furniture.
  • Do not use anchors in a rental wall without asking, unless your lease requires tip-over anchors.
  • Do not play loud music at the wall in return.

Move-out checklist

  • Remove any wall anchors as agreed with the landlord, and fill holes.
  • Take out outlet gaskets if you added them, with the power off.
  • Check the wall behind the bookcase and the bed for scuffs.
  • Remove any felt pads or protectors from the floor.
  • Photograph the shared wall after cleaning.

How-to card

Quiet a bedroom when you hear voices through the wall

The order that tends to help most, from free to cheap.

  • Time: About 1 hour
  • Cost: Low
  • Deposit risk: None

What you need

  • A sound machine with brown noise
  • Foam or silicone earplugs
  • A bookcase or heavy furniture
  • Padded blankets and a sturdy rack (optional)

Steps

  1. Move the bed so your head is not against the shared wall.
  2. Push a full bookcase or dresser against the shared wall.
  3. Hang padded blankets on a rack in front of the wall if you need more.
  4. Put the sound machine between your head and the wall and try brown noise.
  5. Add earplugs or sleep headphones on loud nights.
  6. If the talking is during quiet hours, write a friendly note, then contact management.

Renter note: Ask before anchoring tall furniture. Many leases allow or require anti-tip anchors.

Why can I hear my neighbor talking through the wall?

Many apartment walls are thin, and speech falls in a range they block poorly. Gaps like outlets and baseboards let more sound through.

What is the best way to block voices in a rental?

Move the bed off the shared wall, add a full bookcase or heavy furniture, use brown noise near your head, and wear earplugs if needed.

Do soundproof foam panels help with talking?

Not much. They reduce echo in your room but do little to block sound coming through a wall.

Is brown noise better for blocking voices?

Many people find it covers speech better than white noise. Try both at a comfortable volume.

Can I ask my landlord to soundproof a wall?

You can ask, and you can ask for gaps like outlets and baseboards to be sealed. Full soundproofing is up to the owner.
