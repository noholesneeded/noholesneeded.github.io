How to Cool a Rental With Bad or No AC Without a $500 Power Bill

The short version: Cool a rental with weak or no AC in this order: block the sun during the day with blackout curtains or removable window film, close the windows while it is hotter outside, then pull cooler air in at night with a window fan. Use box fans to move air across you, not only around the room. If you still need AC, cool one room with a correctly sized portable unit and a timer. Measure what it uses with a plug-in power meter.

In an r/Apartmentliving thread on how to cool down with bad or no AC, the poster described 80-degree nights, a power bill of around $500, and ice packs that did not help. That combination is common: the AC runs all day against sun pouring in, and at night the apartment is still holding the heat. The fix is to stop the heat getting in first, then move air at the right times.

Stop the heat getting in

Sun through a window is one of the biggest sources of heat in a room. On sunny windows, especially ones facing west or south:

  • Close blackout curtains during the hottest hours. Thermal-lined blackout curtains block the sun and add a layer between the glass and the room. If you cannot drill, our guide to no-drill curtain rods covers tension rods and brackets.
  • Try removable window film. A removable heat-blocking window film can cut sun through the glass. Choose a static-cling type that goes on with water and peels off later, check the listing before you buy, test a corner first, and check your lease. Some buildings have rules about what can show from outside.
  • Keep the windows closed while it is hotter outside than inside. It feels wrong, but an open window on a 95-degree afternoon lets in hot air.
  • Cut heat from inside. Cook with the microwave or outside the hottest hours, switch to LED bulbs, and turn off electronics you are not using.

Move air when outside is cooler

In the evening, once the outside air is cooler than inside, open up and move air through the apartment.

  1. Put a window fan in one window set to blow in, ideally on the shady side.
  2. Open a window on the other side of the apartment, or set a second fan to blow out.
  3. Close doors to rooms you are not using, so the air moves through the rooms you are in.
  4. Close up again in the morning before it heats up, and close the curtains.

Use a thermometer like the Govee H5075 hygrometer thermometer to see when outside is cooler than inside. You can also check a weather app for the outdoor temperature.

Fans that actually cool you

Fans do not lower the room temperature. They cool your body by moving air across your skin. So point the fan at you. A 20-inch box fan at the foot of the bed is simple and works. The Lasko air circulating box fan is another option for moving air across a room. For more help on a bedroom that runs hotter than the rest of the place, see why your bedroom is hotter than the rest of the apartment.

A damp towel or a cool shower before bed can help you cool down while a fan blows on you. Ice packs in front of a fan help less than people hope.

If you need a portable AC

When fans and curtains are not enough, cooling one room is cheaper than trying to cool the whole apartment. Many leases do not allow window units, but allow a portable AC that vents through a window kit. Check three things before you buy:

  • Size. Match the BTU rating to the room. An 8,000 BTU BLACK+DECKER suits a small bedroom. A 10,000 BTU BLACK+DECKER or the Midea 10,000 BTU unit are for a larger room. Use the room size on the listing as a starting point.
  • Window. Portable ACs need a window kit, and most kits fit sliding or hung windows. Check your window type and width.
  • Outlet. Plug it straight into a wall outlet, not a power strip or a thin extension cord.

Run it only in the room you are in, with the door closed and the curtains drawn. Put it on a timer or a Kasa smart plug so it cools the bedroom before bed instead of running all day. Check that your unit turns back on after a power cut before you use a smart plug.

What it adds to your bill

You do not have to guess. Plug the unit into a Kill A Watt meter and it shows the watts it draws and the kWh it uses. Then do the math with the rate on your bill: kWh used × your rate per kWh = cost. For example, if a unit uses 7 kWh in a night and your bill says 20 cents per kWh, that night cost about $1.40. Thirty nights would be about $42. Your numbers will be different, so measure your own unit and use your own rate.

What not to do

  • Do not leave windows open during the hottest part of the day.
  • Do not run a portable AC on an extension cord or a power strip.
  • Do not install a window unit if your lease bans it. Ask in writing first.
  • Do not try to cool the whole apartment with one portable unit.
  • Do not block a fire escape window with a fan or AC.
  • Do not ignore heat illness. If you feel dizzy, confused, or sick from the heat, get to a cooler place and get help.

Move-out checklist

  • Remove window film slowly and clean the glass.
  • Take out the portable AC window kit and check the sill for scratches or marks.
  • Remove curtain rods and fill holes only as your lease allows.
  • Drain and dry the portable AC before you move it.
  • Photograph the windows and sills after you finish.

How-to card

Cool a bedroom for the night without central AC

A day-to-night routine that uses curtains, fans, and a timer.

  • Time: 20 minutes a day
  • Cost: Low
  • Deposit risk: None

What you need

  • Blackout curtains
  • A window fan
  • A box fan
  • A thermometer
  • A smart plug or timer (optional)

Steps

  1. In the morning, close the windows and draw the curtains on sunny sides.
  2. Keep doors to unused rooms closed.
  3. In the evening, check that outside is cooler than inside.
  4. Open up and set the window fan to blow cooler air in.
  5. Point the box fan at the bed before you sleep.
  6. If you use a portable AC, set it to run for an hour before bed with the door closed.

Renter note: Curtains, film, and fans all come out without damage. Check your lease before adding a window AC.

How do I cool down an apartment without AC?

Block the sun in the day, keep windows closed while it is hotter outside, pull cooler air in at night with a window fan, and point a fan at yourself.

Do blackout curtains help keep a room cool?

Yes. They block sun through the glass, which is a big heat source. Close them during the hottest hours.

Is a portable AC worth it in a rental?

It can be if you cool one closed room and the lease bans window units. Check the window kit, the room size, and the outlet first.

How much does it cost to run a portable AC?

It depends on the unit and your rate. Measure the kWh with a plug-in meter and multiply by the rate on your bill.

Should I put a fan in the window during the day?

Only if outside is cooler than inside. During a hot day, it pulls in hot air.
