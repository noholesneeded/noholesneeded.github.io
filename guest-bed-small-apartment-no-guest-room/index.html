Family Driving In Instead of Flying? Where to Put Guests in a Small Apartment

Your family was going to fly in, stay at a hotel near the airport, and meet you for dinner. Now they are talking about driving instead, and the word "hotel" has quietly dropped out of the plan. You have a one-bedroom or a studio, no guest room, and a lease that does not love surprises. You can still give people a real place to sleep. It takes a bed that fits your floor, a spot for their bag, a way to make the room dark and quiet, and a plan to put it all away after.

Whether your family is driving in this weekend or simply deciding that a car feels safer than an airport this holiday season, the result in a small apartment is the same: more people sleeping over, for more nights, than you planned for. Here is how to set that up without a guest room and without anything that has to be patched later.

Measure first: where can a bed actually go?

The most common mistake is buying the bed first and finding out it blocks the bathroom door. Measure the bed open, not boxed. A queen air mattress is about the size of a queen bed. A twin tri-fold or cot is narrower but still needs a path around it.

  1. Find the floor. The living room is usually the best spot, because it is away from your bedroom and near the bathroom. Move the coffee table to a wall or into the bedroom for the visit.
  2. Tape the outline. Use painter's tape on the floor to mark the bed's open size. Walk the route from the bed to the bathroom and from the bedroom to the kitchen in the dark.
  3. Keep exits clear. Do not put a bed against the front door, across a hallway, or in front of a radiator or space heater.
  4. Check the outlet. A built-in pump needs an outlet within cord reach, and guests will want a place to charge a phone.

Pick the bed for your floor

There is no single best guest bed. The right one depends on how many people, how many nights, and where you will store it the other 11 months.

For two adults: a raised queen air mattress is the usual answer. The SoundAsleep Dream Series queen and the Intex 18-inch queen Dura-Beam Essential Rest both have built-in electric pumps. Inflate it the day before your guests arrive and top it off before bed, since new air beds often feel softer after the first fill.

For one guest: a tri-fold foam mattress needs no pump, folds into a block, and works as a floor seat or ottoman the rest of the year. If your guest has trouble getting up from the floor, an EconoHome folding bed puts the mattress at a more normal height on a cot frame.

Protect the floor. Put a rug, a moving blanket, or a folded comforter under any floor bed. It protects hardwood and laminate, keeps the bed from sliding, and adds a little warmth from a cold floor. Stick felt pads on the feet of a folding cot so it does not scratch the floor when someone gets in.

Pillows and bedding. If you do not keep spares, a set of two standard pillows is the one thing guests notice right away. Make the bed with real sheets and a blanket rather than a sleeping bag, and leave an extra blanket folded at the foot.

Give them privacy without a wall

A guest sleeping in the living room usually wants two things: not to be seen from the kitchen at 6 a.m., and a sense that the corner is theirs. Neither needs a drill.

  • A freestanding screen. A 4-panel folding room divider on wheels stands on its own and rolls into a closet afterward.
  • A no-drill curtain wall. In an alcove or a studio with walls on both sides, a no-drill room divider curtain rod holds a curtain between two walls. Put pads behind the rod ends so they do not mark the paint. Our guide to no-drill room dividers for a studio covers the options in more detail.
  • A bed tent. For a twin-size bed or cot, an Alvantor privacy pop-up bed tent gives a guest their own dark, enclosed space without touching the walls at all.

Make it dark and quiet

Living rooms are the brightest, loudest room in most apartments: street light, the refrigerator, a hallway light under the door. Guests who did not sleep well remember it longer than anything else about the visit.

  • Darken the window. If the guest sleeps by a bright window, custom-size blackout curtains from TWOPAGES can be ordered to fit that window and moved with you later. For a recessed window with no drilling allowed, hang them on a tension rod. Our guide to blackout curtains that actually block light explains why edges leak and how to stop it.
  • Hand them a mask. A Manta sleep mask blocks the light the curtains cannot, like a hallway light or a phone charging.
  • Cover the noise. A LectroFan Classic sound machine on the floor near the bed masks the refrigerator, the radiator, and your own early alarm.

If your building is loud in general, see what to do when white noise is not enough.

Give their stuff a place

The other thing that makes a small apartment feel cramped with guests is luggage open on the floor. Two small additions fix most of it.

  • A luggage rack. A folding luggage rack holds an open suitcase at waist height and folds flat against a wall afterward.
  • Over-the-door hooks. Over-the-door hooks on the bathroom or closet door give coats, towels, and tomorrow's clothes a spot, with no adhesive and no holes.
  • A mirror. If your only mirror is in the bathroom, an over-the-door full-length mirror lets guests get ready without waiting for the bathroom.
  • A towel set and a basket. Leave a set of towels, a spare phone charger, and a glass for water on a stool or chair next to the bed.

If your entry is tiny, our guide to shoe and coat storage with no entryway has more ways to deal with extra boots and coats.

Before they arrive, sleep one night on the guest bed yourself. You will find the draft, the streetlight, and the squeaky floorboard before they do.

Check the lease before a long stay

A weekend visit is normal. A guest who stays for weeks can be a lease question, because many leases have a guest or occupancy clause. If your family might stay longer than a few nights, read that clause, and if it is unclear, a short email to the property manager is better than a surprise. Keep the reply.

After they leave

  • Deflate the air mattress fully and let it dry before folding, then store it in its bag.
  • Wash the bedding and store it in an under-bed storage bag so it is ready next time.
  • Fold the divider, luggage rack, and cot and put them in a closet.
  • Lift the tension rod down and wipe any marks where the ends sat.
  • Check the floor under the bed for scuffs and the door tops for rubbing from hooks.

Set up a guest sleeping spot in a small apartment

Measure the floor, choose the bed, add privacy and darkness, and give guests a place for their things, with no drilling.

  • Time: About 1 hour
  • Cost: Low to moderate
  • Deposit risk: Low

What you need

  • painter's tape and a tape measure
  • an air mattress, tri-fold foam mattress, or folding cot
  • a rug or blanket for under the bed
  • a folding screen or no-drill divider rod and curtain
  • blackout curtains or a sleep mask
  • a white noise machine
  • a luggage rack and over-the-door hooks

Steps

  1. Tape the open bed outline on the floor and check the path to the bathroom and the exits.
  2. Choose a queen air mattress for two adults, or a tri-fold or cot for one guest.
  3. Put a rug or blanket under the bed and inflate an air mattress the day before.
  4. Set up a folding screen or tension-rod curtain for privacy.
  5. Darken the window and put a white noise machine near the bed.
  6. Add a luggage rack, over-the-door hooks, towels, and a phone charger.
  7. Sleep on it one night yourself and fix what bothers you.

Renter note: Do not block exits or heaters with a bed, and check your lease's guest clause before a long stay.

Where do guests sleep in a small apartment with no guest room?

Usually in the living room, on a queen air mattress, a tri-fold foam mattress, or a folding cot, with a folding screen or curtain for privacy. Keep exits, hallways, and heaters clear.

Is an air mattress or a tri-fold mattress better for guests?

A raised queen air mattress fits two adults and stores small, but needs an outlet and topping off. A tri-fold foam mattress fits one person, needs no pump, and doubles as floor seating between visits.

How do I give a guest privacy in a studio apartment?

Use a freestanding folding room divider, a no-drill tension-rod curtain between two walls, or a pop-up bed tent. None of them needs anything attached to the walls.

Will an air mattress damage hardwood floors?

Put a rug, moving blanket, or comforter under it to protect the floor and stop it sliding, and add felt pads to the feet of any folding bed frame.

Can guests stay in my rental for the holidays?

Short visits are normal, but many leases have a guest or occupancy clause for longer stays. Read yours, and ask the property manager in writing if it is unclear.
