Why the Litter Box Still Smells After You Scoop Daily (and What to Change)

The short version: When a litter box smells even with daily scooping, the cause is usually something left behind: urine soaked into the bottom of shallow litter, a scratched plastic box that holds odor, clumps that break apart in the scoop, too few boxes, or a corner with no airflow. Check those first. Use about 2 to 3 inches of unscented clumping litter, a rigid metal scoop, and a full change on a schedule. A sudden new smell can be a health sign, so call the vet.

A post on r/CatAdvice asked, simply, why the litter box still smells even when they scoop daily. The replies came back to the same few things: the box, the litter depth, and how often the whole box gets changed. If you have not read our main guide on how to stop litter box smell in a one-bedroom apartment, start there for the full setup. This post is the troubleshooting list for when you already scoop every day.

1. Check the box itself

Plastic boxes get scratched by claws and scoops. Urine and bacteria settle in the scratches, and the box starts to smell even when it is clean. If your box is more than a year old, or if it smells after washing, it may be time to replace it. A non-porous box, like a stainless steel box, does not scratch the same way. Wash any box with mild, unscented soap and water when you do a full change. Avoid strong scented cleaners and bleach mixes, because some cats avoid a box that smells of them.

2. Check the litter depth

If the litter is too shallow, urine reaches the bottom of the box and spreads out instead of clumping. That layer stays behind after you scoop. Common advice is to keep about 2 to 3 inches of clumping litter in the box, and to top it up after scooping so it stays at that depth.

3. Check the litter

Scented litters can mix perfume with the waste smell instead of removing it, and some cats dislike them. Many people do better with an unscented clumping litter, like Dr. Elsey’s Ultra or World’s Best Cat Litter unscented. If you change litters, mix the new one into the old over about a week so your cat accepts it.

4. Check how you scoop

Thin plastic scoops flex and break clumps. The broken bits fall back into the litter and smell later. A stiff stainless steel scoop or an aluminum sifter scoop lifts the clump in one piece. Scoop along the bottom and the sides, where urine clumps stick.

Scoop at least once a day, and twice if you can. Bag the clumps and take them to an outdoor bin, or use a sealed pail like the Litter Genie, so the smell does not move from the box to the kitchen trash.

5. Check how often you change it all

Scooping removes most of the waste, but not all of it. Fine bits and soaked litter build up over time. Many owners empty the box completely, wash it, and refill it every 2 to 4 weeks with clumping litter, and more often with non-clumping. If the smell starts to build at the same point each month, change it a few days earlier.

6. Check the number of boxes

A common rule from vets and shelters is one box per cat, plus one. Two cats sharing one box means more waste in the same litter, which smells faster. If you have the space, add a second box in a different spot.

7. Check the floor and mat around it

Sometimes the smell is not in the box at all. It is on the floor, the mat, or the wall beside it, from a cat that kicks litter or pees over the edge. Clean the area with an enzyme cleaner like Nature’s Miracle, which is made to break down urine. Wash or replace the litter mat. A box with higher sides can help with cats that spray over the rim.

8. Check the location and air

A box in a closed closet, a bathroom with no fan, or a dead corner has no air movement, so the smell builds up. Try a spot with some airflow, away from where you eat and sleep but not hidden in a cupboard. An air purifier like the Levoit Core 300 can help with smell that is already in the air. It will not fix a box that needs cleaning. A baking soda litter deodorizer can help between changes. Use it lightly, as some cats do not like strong-smelling litter.

9. Check your cat

If the smell is new, much stronger than usual, or very sharp like ammonia, it can be a sign of a health problem, like a urinary issue or a change in diet. Call your vet if you notice:

  • More or bigger urine clumps than usual.
  • Peeing outside the box.
  • Straining in the box, blood in the urine, or crying while peeing.
  • A sudden change in the smell of the urine or stool.

A male cat that strains to pee and produces little or nothing needs a vet urgently.

What not to do

  • Do not cover the smell with plug-in air fresheners or scented sprays near the box.
  • Do not clean the box with ammonia-based cleaners. Urine already smells like ammonia, and the smell can draw your cat back to the wrong spot.
  • Do not mix bleach with other cleaners.
  • Do not put the box in a sealed cupboard with no air.
  • Do not ignore a sudden change in smell or litter habits.

Move-out checklist

  • Clean the floor, baseboards, and wall around the box with an enzyme cleaner.
  • Check carpet near the box with a flashlight for stains, and treat them early.
  • Wash or throw out the old litter mat.
  • Air out the room and change the purifier filter if it is due.
  • Photograph the area after cleaning.

How-to card

Do a full litter-box change

The full empty-and-refill that daily scooping cannot replace.

  • Time: 30 minutes
  • Cost: Low
  • Deposit risk: Low

What you need

  • Fresh unscented clumping litter
  • A stiff metal scoop
  • Mild unscented soap
  • Gloves
  • A trash bag
  • An enzyme cleaner for the floor

Steps

  1. Empty all the litter into a trash bag and tie it closed.
  2. Wash the box with mild unscented soap and warm water, and dry it completely.
  3. Clean the floor, mat, and nearby wall with an enzyme cleaner.
  4. Refill the box with 2 to 3 inches of fresh litter.
  5. Put the box back in a spot with some airflow.
  6. Write the date on your calendar for the next full change.

Renter note: Treat any urine near the box early. Old stains on rental carpet are harder to remove at move-out.

Why does my litter box smell even though I scoop every day?

Common causes are a scratched plastic box, litter that is too shallow, broken clumps, scented litter, not changing the box often enough, too few boxes, or urine on the floor nearby.

How deep should cat litter be?

Common advice is about 2 to 3 inches of clumping litter, topped up after scooping.

How often should I change all the litter?

Many owners do a full change every 2 to 4 weeks with clumping litter. Change it sooner if the smell builds early.

Does a stainless steel litter box smell less?

It does not scratch like plastic, so it is less likely to hold onto odor over time.

When is litter box smell a sign of a health problem?

If the smell is suddenly stronger, there are more clumps, or your cat strains or pees outside the box, call your vet.
