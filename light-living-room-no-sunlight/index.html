How to Light a Living Room With No Sunlight (or No Window)

A thread on r/NYCapartments asked how to deal with a living room with no sunlight. The advice in threads like it is steady: more lamps, warmer bulbs, mirrors, and leaving the overhead light off more often than you would think. This guide turns that into a plan for a rental where you cannot add wiring.

Why one overhead light makes it worse

A single ceiling fixture lights the middle of the floor and leaves the walls and corners dark. In a room with no daylight, dark walls are what make it feel small. Bright light from one point can also cause glare and harsh shadows, which is why the room can feel brighter but less pleasant. The fix is to light the surfaces: the ceiling, the walls, and the corners. That is called layered lighting, and it is how designers light rooms that never get sun.

How much light do you need?

For a rough rule of thumb, many lighting guides suggest around 10 to 20 lumens per square foot for a living room. A 12-by-14-foot room is 168 square feet, so that is about 1,700 to 3,400 lumens across all the lamps. A standard 60-watt-equivalent LED bulb gives around 800 lumens, so a dark living room often needs light from three or four sources. Check the lumen number on the box rather than the watt number.

Bounce light off the walls and ceiling

Start with the darkest corner. An uplight, like the Brightech Eclipse torchiere, sends light to the ceiling, and the ceiling spreads it across the room. This makes the room feel taller and less closed in. If your ceiling is white or light, it works best.

On the opposite side, add a lamp at seat height next to the sofa or a reading chair. A three-head tree floor lamp can light a corner and a chair at once. A table lamp on a side table works too. For the walls, plug-in sconces add light at eye level without an electrician. Mount them with permission using the screws they come with, or ask your landlord. Some renters hang them from heavy-duty adhesive hooks rated for the weight. Hide the cord in a paintable cord cover if you want a cleaner look.

Choose warm, matching bulbs

In a room with no daylight, cool white bulbs can make the space feel like an office. Warm white, around 2700K, feels more like evening sun. Use the same color in every lamp, so the room does not mix yellow and blue light. The Philips soft white 2700K 8-pack makes that easy. If you want brighter light in the morning and warmer light at night, smart A19 bulbs can switch color temperature from an app. Keep them in one or two lamps rather than every fixture.

Turn the lamps on before you need them. In a room with no window, a lamp on a smart plug that comes on at 4 p.m. or when you wake up makes the room feel less like a cave.

Add a mirror and lighter fabrics

A large mirror does not make light, but it bounces the light you have. Lean an arched floor mirror with a stand across from your main lamp, or beside it, so you see the light twice. You do not need to hang it.

Then look at fabrics. Dark sofas, rugs, and curtains soak up light. A light throw on the sofa, a lighter rug, and pale cushions help the room look brighter with the same lamps. If you can paint with permission, a light warm white wall helps. If you cannot, a light rug and big lampshades do some of the same work. If the room has no window at all

Some living rooms in converted or railroad apartments have no window, or only a window onto an interior hall. In that case:

  • Light the room like it is always evening. Plan for all-day lamp use, so pick LED bulbs and lamps you like the look of.
  • Use a brighter, cooler light in the morning if it helps you wake up. A smart bulb that starts brighter and shifts warmer later can make the day feel less flat.
  • Fake a window. A sheer curtain over a blank wall, lit from behind or above by a corner lamp, can soften a wall and give the eye a place to rest.
  • Get daylight elsewhere. If a dark room drags your mood down, spend some time near a real window or outside each day. If it is affecting your sleep or mood a lot, talk to a doctor.

What not to do

  • Do not rely on one bright overhead fixture.
  • Do not mix cool and warm bulbs in the same room.
  • Do not hang a heavy mirror or sconce on adhesive without checking the weight rating.
  • Do not run lamp cords under rugs where they can get damaged and overheat.
  • Do not overload one outlet or power strip with many lamps and heaters together.

Move-out checklist

  • Remove sconces and any mounting hardware, and fill holes only as your lease allows.
  • Take down cord covers slowly, and test any adhesive remover on a hidden spot first.
  • Put back the original bulbs in ceiling fixtures if you changed them.
  • Check walls behind lamps and the mirror for scuffs.
  • Photograph each wall in good light after you finish.

How-to card

Layer the lighting in a living room with no sun

Three light heights, matching bulbs, and a timer, set up in an hour.

  • Time: 1 hour
  • Cost: Varies
  • Deposit risk: Low

What you need

  • A torchiere or corner floor lamp
  • One or two lamps for seat height
  • Warm 2700K LED bulbs
  • Smart plugs
  • A large mirror (optional)

Steps

  1. Find the darkest corner and put the uplight there.
  2. Place a lamp at seat height on the far side of the room.
  3. Put matching 2700K bulbs in every lamp.
  4. Lean a large mirror across from or beside the main lamp.
  5. Set the lamps on smart plugs to turn on at the time the room gets dark.
  6. Turn off the overhead light and check the room in the evening.

Renter note: Nothing here needs wiring. If you want to mount plug-in sconces, ask the landlord about screw holes first.

How do I brighten a living room with no natural light?

Use several lamps at different heights, warm matching bulbs, and a large mirror to bounce the light. Light the ceiling and corners, not only the middle of the room.

What color bulb is best for a dark living room?

Warm white, around 2700K, usually feels best in the evening. Some people use brighter, cooler light in the morning with a smart bulb.

How many lamps does a dark living room need?

Often three or four. A common rule of thumb is about 10 to 20 lumens per square foot for a living room.

Can I install wall sconces in a rental?

Plug-in sconces do not need wiring. Mounting them usually takes a couple of screws, so check your lease or ask the landlord.

Do mirrors really make a dark room brighter?

They do not add light, but they bounce lamp light back into the room, which can make it feel brighter and bigger.
