A Deposit-Safe Rental Bathroom Makeover (Everything Comes Back Off)

Almond tile, a plastic shower curtain with a mildew line at the bottom, a dripping shower head, and a single bare bulb over the mirror. Rental bathrooms tend to be the least loved room in the apartment, and the one you use every single morning. You can change how it looks and feels a lot, and take every bit of it with you when you leave.

The short version: For a deposit-safe rental bathroom makeover, start with the things that come back off: a new fabric shower curtain on metal glide hooks, a handheld shower head that screws onto the existing arm (keep the old one), matching towels and a bath mat, warm LED bulbs, and freestanding storage. Add removable wallpaper or peel-and-stick tile only after testing on the paint and away from direct water. Take photos before you start, and put everything back at move-out.

On r/HomeDecorating and r/femalelivingspace, renters share before-and-after photos of bathrooms they could not renovate. The biggest changes nearly always come from textiles, light, and the shower head, not from paint or tile.

Before you start

  • Take dated photos of the whole bathroom, close up, including any damage.
  • Read your lease for rules on fixtures, wallpaper, and paint.
  • Ask your landlord about anything you would change beyond textiles, like the shower head, wallpaper, or the mirror. A short message with product links usually gets a quick answer.
  • Keep every original piece you remove, in a labeled bag, in a closet.

Shower curtain and hooks

The shower curtain is the largest surface you can change. A waffle weave curtain looks softer and more finished than plastic. Add a washable fabric liner behind it, and wash both often to prevent mildew. Double glide hooks slide more smoothly than plastic rings and look neater. If the rod is too short or too low, a taller tension rod can raise the curtain, which makes the room look taller.

A better shower head

A handheld shower head like the AquaCare usually screws onto the existing arm. Many come with the parts you need, and the swap takes a few minutes with an adjustable wrench and plumber’s tape. Check the listing to be sure. Keep the original head. At move-out, swap it back. Use a cloth around the nut so the wrench does not scratch the finish, and do not overtighten.

Before swapping the shower head, check for leaks. If the arm or the valve leaks or feels loose, stop and tell your landlord. A plumbing problem is their repair.

Textiles and mats

Matching towels, a bath mat, and a hand towel in one or two colors make the room look planned. A non-slip tub mat makes the tub safer and covers stained or worn enamel. Lift it out and let it dry after showers so mildew does not grow underneath. If the wall towel bar is short, a freestanding towel rack gives towels room to dry.

Light

Swap cool, harsh bulbs for warm 2700K LED bulbs, if the fixture takes standard bulbs. Keep the old bulbs to put back if you need to. A warmer, brighter light changes the feel of a bathroom more than most decor.

Wallpaper and tile

Removable wallpaper like NuWallpaper can transform a small bathroom wall. But bathrooms are humid, and adhesive behaves differently there.

  • Use it on a wall away from direct water, like behind the toilet or on the wall opposite the shower.
  • Test a piece on the paint for a week first, then peel a corner slowly.
  • Make sure the bathroom has a working fan or a window, so the paper is not soaked with steam daily.
  • Do not put it on freshly painted walls.

For a tile backsplash behind the sink, see peel-and-stick backsplash, which covers the same surface checks.

Pick a simple color plan

In a small bathroom with tile you cannot change, the tile is your starting point. Work with it rather than against it:

  • Beige, almond, or pink tile. Warm whites, cream, olive, rust, or soft terracotta towels tend to sit well next to warm tile. Stark bright white can make old tile look more yellow.
  • Gray or blue tile. Crisp white, navy, black accents, or a muted green work.
  • Plain white tile. Almost anything works. Pick one strong color and repeat it in two or three places.
  • Busy or patterned tile. Keep everything else plain, in one solid color, so the room looks calm.

Repeat each color at least twice, like the curtain and the towels, or the mat and a soap dish. That repetition is what makes a room look planned instead of collected.

Small finishing touches

  • A soap dispenser and a tray in one material clear the sink edge of bottles.
  • A plant that likes humidity, like a pothos, on a shelf near the window, if there is light.
  • New knobs on a vanity, only if you keep the originals and your landlord agrees.
  • A framed print hung with picture hanging strips on a dry wall away from steam, or leaned on a shelf.
  • A lidded basket for spare toilet paper and cleaning supplies, instead of open packs on the floor.

Storage

Clutter makes any bathroom look worse. Freestanding storage and tension caddies give everything a home without drilling. See small bathroom storage with no drilling for the options.

What not to do

  • Do not paint, regrout, or recaulk without permission.
  • Do not throw away the original shower head, bulbs, or curtain rod.
  • Do not put wallpaper in direct shower spray.
  • Do not overtighten plumbing connections.
  • Do not drill into tile to hang shelves without written permission.

Move-out checklist

  • Put back the original shower head with plumber’s tape and check for drips.
  • Put back the original bulbs, if needed.
  • Remove wallpaper slowly and clean any residue.
  • Take your curtain, hooks, mats, and towel rack.
  • Clean the tub, tile, and grout, and take photos.

How-to card

Make over a rental bathroom without losing the deposit

Big change, nothing permanent, everything goes back.

  • Time: About 3 hours
  • Cost: Low
  • Deposit risk: Low

What you need

  • A fabric curtain and liner
  • Glide hooks
  • A handheld shower head
  • An adjustable wrench and a cloth
  • Plumber’s tape
  • Warm LED bulbs
  • Removable wallpaper for a test

Steps

  1. Take photos and ask your landlord about fixture changes.
  2. Hang a fabric curtain and liner on glide hooks.
  3. Swap the shower head, and bag and label the original.
  4. Change the bulbs to warm 2700K LEDs.
  5. Add matching towels and a mat.
  6. Test removable wallpaper on a dry wall for a week.
  7. Add freestanding storage.

Renter note: If the bathroom has mold or no fan, fix the moisture first. See our guide to bathrooms with no exhaust fan.

How can I make my rental bathroom look better without losing my deposit?

Change what comes back off: the shower curtain and hooks, the shower head, towels and mats, bulbs, and freestanding storage. Test removable wallpaper before using it.

Can I change the shower head in a rental?

Often yes, if you keep the original and put it back. Ask your landlord, and report any leaks.

Does peel-and-stick wallpaper work in a bathroom?

It can on walls away from direct water, in a bathroom with good ventilation. Test it on the paint for a week first.

What is the cheapest way to update a rental bathroom?

A new shower curtain and hooks, matching towels, and warm bulbs.

How do I cover an ugly bathtub in a rental?

A non-slip tub mat covers worn enamel inside the tub, and a longer curtain can hide the outside.
