Drafty Windows in a Rental: How to Stop the Cold Air This Winter

You can feel it with your hand: a thin, cold stream of air along the bottom of the window. The radiator clanks away, the thermostat says 68, and the couch by the window still feels like it is outside. Old rental windows leak, and you usually cannot replace them. You can, however, close most of the leaks for the winter.

The short version: To stop drafty windows in a rental, find the leaks, then seal them with removable materials. Press caulk cord into gaps around the sash and frame, add foam tape where the window closes, and lay a draft stopper on the sill. For the glass, add a shrink film kit. Hang thermal curtains on a wraparound rod and close them at night. Lock the window tight, and tell your landlord in writing about broken locks, cracked panes, or gaps you cannot seal.

On r/NYCapartments and r/povertyfinance, renters trade the same short list every winter: rope caulk, plastic film, curtains, and a towel at the sill. They are cheap, removable, and they add up. Here is how to do them well.

Find the drafts

Pick a cold, windy day and check each window:

  • Hold a strip of tissue or a light ribbon near the edges of the sash, the frame, and the meeting rail between sashes. Where it moves, air is getting in.
  • Feel with the back of your hand. Cold air is easy to feel.
  • Check the window locks. A window that does not lock tight leaves a gap.
  • Look for cracked glass, missing putty, or broken sash cords.
  • Check around a window AC unit, if you have one.

Mark the spots with small pieces of painter’s tape so you know where to seal.

Seal the gaps

  1. Lock it. On double-hung windows, the lock pulls the sashes together and closes the meeting gap.
  2. Caulk cord. Press Mortite into gaps around the sash and frame with your fingers. It stays soft and peels off. Test on painted trim first, since it can leave a slight mark on some paint.
  3. Foam tape. Stick foam weatherseal where the sash closes against the frame. Clean the surface first. It may leave adhesive when removed, so test, and use it only where you can clean it off.
  4. Sill draft stopper. A draft snake along the sill blocks air from the bottom of the sash.
  5. Do not seal a window shut if you need it for a fire exit, or for a window AC or a fan in summer.

Add a film kit

A shrink film kit adds a clear plastic layer inside the window frame. You stick double-sided tape around the frame, press on the film, and use a hair dryer to tighten it. It creates a pocket of still air and stops drafts at the same time. Some tips:

  • Clean and dry the frame first.
  • Put the tape on wood or a smooth frame, not on fresh or flaking paint. Test a small piece first, since the tape can pull paint off at removal.
  • Measure and cut the film with a few inches extra on each side.
  • Remove it slowly in spring, pulling the tape back at a low angle.

Apply film kits on a mild, dry day if you can. The tape sticks better to a dry, room-temperature frame than to a cold, damp one.

Curtains

Thermal or lined blackout curtains add a layer between you and the cold glass. Close them at night and on cloudy days. Open them on sunny days to let the sun warm the room. Coverage matters most. Curtains should reach the sill or floor and cover the frame on both sides. A wraparound rod lets the curtain curve back to the wall to block air at the edges. If you cannot drill for a rod, see no-drill curtain rods.

Other drafts in the same room

  • Outlets on outside walls. Foam gaskets behind outlet and switch covers can stop small drafts. Turn off power at the breaker, unscrew the cover, fit the gasket, and put the cover back.
  • Doors. A door sweep or a draft stopper at the bottom of an exterior door helps.
  • Baseboards. Gaps under baseboards on outside walls can leak. Ask your landlord about sealing them.

Use the sun and the heat well

Sealing is half of it. The other half is making the most of the heat you have:

  • Open curtains on sunny days, especially on south- and west-facing windows, then close them as soon as the sun goes down.
  • Keep furniture off radiators and vents, so warm air can move into the room.
  • Pull the sofa or bed a little away from the coldest window. Sitting right next to cold glass feels colder than the room really is.
  • Close doors to rooms you do not use, so the heat stays where you are.

Watch for condensation

Sealing drafts makes a room warmer, but it can also trap moisture. Watch for water on the glass in the morning. A hygrometer by the window helps. If condensation builds up, see our guide to window condensation. Opening the curtains in the day and running a bathroom fan help.

Tell your landlord

In writing, report:

  • Windows that do not close or lock.
  • Cracked or broken glass.
  • Missing putty, rotted frames, or broken sash cords.
  • Large gaps you cannot seal.
  • A room that will not stay warm even with the heat on.

Many places have rules about heat and window repair in rentals. Check local tenant resources if repairs do not happen.

What not to do

  • Do not seal a window you need for fire exit.
  • Do not use permanent caulk without permission.
  • Do not put film tape on flaking or fresh paint.
  • Do not cover heating vents or radiators with curtains.
  • Do not ignore broken locks or glass. Report them.

Move-out checklist

  • Peel out caulk cord.
  • Remove film kits and tape slowly; clean residue.
  • Remove foam tape and clean the frame.
  • Take down curtains and rods; patch holes if allowed.
  • Reinstall outlet covers without gaskets if the landlord prefers.

How do I stop drafts from my rental windows?

Lock them tight, press removable caulk cord into gaps, add foam tape and a sill draft stopper, and add a shrink film kit and thermal curtains.

Are window film kits safe for rentals?

Usually, if you test the tape first and remove it slowly. Avoid putting tape on fresh or flaking paint.

Is rope caulk removable?

Yes. It stays soft and peels out. Test on painted trim first.

Do thermal curtains really help?

They add a layer between you and cold glass. They help most when they cover the whole window and close at night.

Should my landlord fix drafty windows?

Broken locks, cracked glass, and damaged frames are usually repairs. Report them in writing and check local rules.
