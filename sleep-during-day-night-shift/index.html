How to Sleep During the Day on Night Shift: Light, Noise, and Heat

The short version: Day sleep after a night shift goes better when the bedroom acts like night: dark at the edges of the window, steady sound to cover daytime noise, and a cool room. Use blackout curtains on a wraparound rod, an eye mask for whatever light gets through, a fan or sound machine plus earplugs, and a fan pointed at the bed. Tell the people around you when you sleep. If you still cannot sleep after all that, talk to a doctor.

On r/Nurses, one thread on how night-shift nurses sleep during the day included a reply from someone who had “tried everything over the past 25 years.” On r/Nightshift, a poster said they were absolutely unable to sleep during the day, capped at about four hours. Not every problem is fixable with a bedroom setup. But light, noise, and heat are the three things you can control, and they are where most of the useful replies start.

Make the room dark at noon

Daytime light is the hardest part. Even good blackout curtains leak light at the top, the sides, and the middle if the panels do not overlap.

  • Use real blackout fabric. Hold the curtain up to a bright window. If you can see light through the fabric, it is room-darkening, not blackout. Our guide to blackout curtains that actually block light goes deeper.
  • Close the sides. A wraparound rod bends back to the wall, so light cannot slip around the edges.
  • Overlap the middle. Use panels wide enough to overlap by a few inches, or clip them together.
  • Cover the top. Mount the rod higher and wider than the window, or add a valance.
  • No drilling? RYB HOME no-drill adhesive blackout curtains stick around the frame. Test the adhesive on a hidden part of the frame first.

Even then, some light gets through. A Manta sleep mask has adjustable eye cups that block light without pressing on your eyes. Use it every day, not only when the room is bright, so it becomes part of the routine.

Cover daytime noise

Daytime is louder than night. Traffic, school buses, leaf blowers, and neighbors are all awake. Two layers work well:

  1. A steady sound. A LectroFan Classic or a Yogasleep Dohm covers many daytime sounds. Put it between the bed and the noisiest wall or the door.
  2. Earplugs. Soft foam earplugs take the edge off sudden sounds the machine cannot cover.

For neighbor noise that gets through anyway, see what to do when white noise isn’t enough.

Silence your phone’s notifications during sleep hours but keep emergency contacts able to ring through. Most phones let you set that up in sleep or focus mode.

Keep the room cool in the afternoon

Many bedrooms heat up most in the afternoon, right when day sleepers are in the deepest part of their sleep. Blackout curtains already help by blocking sun. A box fan pointed at the bed adds airflow. If your bedroom runs hot, our guide to why your bedroom is hotter than the rest of the apartment and how to cool a rental with bad or no AC have more ideas.

Tell the household and the building

A good room setup can still be ruined by a knock. A few habits help:

  • Hang a do-not-disturb sign on the bedroom door, and tell roommates or family your sleep hours.
  • Ask for packages to be left without knocking, or have them sent to a locker or a pickup point on your sleep days.
  • Put your shift calendar where the household can see it.
  • If a building has scheduled work, like hallway cleaning or maintenance, ask management which days it is so you can plan.

A steady routine helps too

These are general sleep habits that many shift workers use. They are not medical advice.

  • Keep the same sleep window on work days, as much as you can.
  • Wear sunglasses on the way home if the morning light wakes you up.
  • Avoid big meals and caffeine late in the shift.
  • Use a smart plug to turn the fan or sound machine on when you get home, so the routine starts without you thinking about it.

If you still sleep only a few hours a day, are drowsy at work or while driving, or feel unwell, talk to a doctor. Shift work can cause sleep problems that need medical help.

A sample day after a 7 p.m. to 7 a.m. shift

Here is one way to lay out the day. Shift times vary, so move the times to fit yours.

  • 7:00 to 8:00 a.m. Drive or ride home. Sunglasses on if the light bothers you. Skip the coffee on the way.
  • 8:00 a.m. A light snack if you are hungry, a quick shower, and the phone into sleep mode. The smart plug has already turned on the fan and sound machine.
  • 8:30 a.m. Curtains closed, mask on, earplugs in. The door sign is up.
  • 3:30 or 4:00 p.m. Wake up, open the curtains, and get some daylight.
  • Before your next shift. A real meal and whatever helps you feel awake.

On days off, many people keep their sleep close to this window, or shift it a little at a time, rather than flipping back to a day schedule all at once. Find what works for your body and your household.

What not to do

  • Do not tape foil or trash bags to the window. They can leave residue and are not allowed in many buildings.
  • Do not rely on alcohol or over-the-counter sleep aids without talking to a doctor.
  • Do not drive drowsy after a night shift. If you are struggling to stay awake, get a ride or rest first.
  • Do not block a fire exit window or the smoke detector to make the room darker or quieter.
  • Do not sleep with earplugs if you have no other way to hear an alarm.

Move-out checklist

  • Remove adhesive curtains slowly and clean any residue from the frame.
  • Take down rods and fill holes only as your lease allows.
  • Check the window frame and sill for marks.
  • Put back the landlord’s blinds or curtains if you took them down.
  • Photograph the windows after you finish.

How-to card

Set up a bedroom for day sleep

Light, noise, and heat, set up in one afternoon before your first night shift.

  • Time: About 1 hour
  • Cost: Varies
  • Deposit risk: Low

What you need

  • Blackout curtains
  • A wraparound rod or no-drill adhesive curtains
  • A sleep mask
  • A sound machine
  • Foam earplugs
  • A box fan
  • A door sign

Steps

  1. Hang blackout curtains so they cover the top, sides, and middle of the window.
  2. Check the room at midday and fix any light leaks.
  3. Put the sound machine between the bed and the noisiest wall or door.
  4. Point a fan at the bed.
  5. Set your phone to sleep mode for your sleep hours.
  6. Hang the door sign and tell the household your schedule.

Renter note: Choose no-drill curtains or a tension rod if your lease bans holes, and test adhesive on a hidden spot first.

How do night shift workers sleep during the day?

Most make the bedroom dark, quiet, and cool, keep the same sleep window, and tell the household their schedule. Blackout curtains, a sleep mask, a sound machine, earplugs, and a fan are the common tools.

What is the best way to block daylight for sleep?

Use real blackout fabric on a wraparound rod, overlap the panels, cover the top, and wear a sleep mask for anything left.

Does white noise help with daytime sleep?

It can cover steady sounds like traffic and voices. Pair it with earplugs for sudden noises.

How do I keep my bedroom cool for day sleep?

Close blackout curtains, point a fan at the bed, and cool the room before you sleep if you have AC.

When should a night shift worker see a doctor about sleep?

If you sleep only a few hours a day, are drowsy at work or driving, or feel unwell despite a good setup, talk to a doctor.
