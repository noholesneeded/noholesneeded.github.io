Laundry Storage in a Small Apartment: Hampers, Carts, and Where the Detergent Goes

In a small apartment, laundry never really has a home. Dirty clothes pile on a chair because the hamper does not fit anywhere, the detergent jug lives on the bathroom floor, and the clean load sits in a basket for three days because there is nowhere to fold it. If your laundry is down the hall, in the basement, or at a laundromat two blocks away, you also have to carry all of it there and back. You cannot build a laundry nook or screw shelves above the machine in a rental, but you can give each part of the job a spot that takes very little floor, comes apart at move-out, and does not leave marks.

The short version: Put the hamper where you actually get undressed, and pick its shape for the space you have: a slim rolling hamper for a gap beside the dresser, a hanging bag on a door if you have no floor at all, or a two-section hamper if you sort as you go. Keep wet towels out of the hamper until they dry. Store detergent up high and in its original container, especially pods if kids or pets are around. If you have a washer in the unit, use magnetic shelves on its side or a freestanding frame over it instead of drilling. If you carry laundry out, a backpack laundry bag and a collapsible basket make the trip easier and fold away after.

Where to keep the hamper is one of the most common questions renters ask once they move somewhere small. In a r/AskNYC thread from a renter who had just moved into a studio, one person said they kept a laundry backpack hanging on the outside of the closet door on an over-door hook, so it was ready to carry to the laundromat when it filled up. Another kept a basket in the closet, and when they had even less room, a laundry bag under the bed. In r/Homeorganization, several people said the best spot is wherever you take your clothes off, and others warned against a bathroom with no fan, where the humidity can leave dirty clothes damp and musty. Those threads point to the three questions that matter: where do clothes come off, how do they get to the machine, and where does the clean load go.

Step 1: Put the hamper where clothes come off

A hamper that sits across the apartment does not get used. Clothes land on the chair, the bed, or the floor next to where you change. Start by noticing where that pile forms, then put the hamper there, even if it is not where you think a hamper should go. You can move it to a better spot later once the habit is set.

In a small apartment, the usual options are:

  • Inside the closet, on the floor at one end, if there is a gap beside the shoes or under shorter hanging clothes. Leave the door open a crack or use a hamper with mesh sides so air moves.
  • Beside the dresser or nightstand, where a slim hamper fits in a gap of 10 to 12 inches.
  • On the back of the bedroom or bathroom door, in a hanging bag, if there is no floor at all.
  • In the bathroom, only if it has a working exhaust fan or a window. A steamy bathroom with no airflow is the worst place for a hamper. Our guide to a bathroom with no exhaust fan explains why.

Measure the gap before you buy, including depth. A hamper that is 2 inches too deep will stick out past the dresser and end up in the walkway, where you trip on it and eventually move it somewhere you do not use it.

Step 2: Pick the hamper shape for your space

Most small-apartment hamper problems are shape problems, not size problems. A big round basket wastes the corners of a closet and blocks a narrow gap. Match the hamper to the space you found in Step 1.

Slim rolling hampers for narrow gaps

A slim hamper with wheels and a lid is designed for the gap between furniture and the wall, or the end of a closet. The wheels let you roll it out to the door on laundry day instead of carrying a heavy basket across the room, and the lid hides the clothes if the hamper is in view. A slim hamper fills faster than a wide one. That can be a good thing in a small space, because it nudges you to run smaller loads more often instead of letting a mountain build up.

Hanging hampers when there is no floor

If every inch of floor is already used, take the hamper off the floor. A hanging hamper bag hooks over the top of a door or around a closet rod. Before you hang one, close the door slowly to check that the hooks do not stop it from latching. If the hooks rub the top edge of the door, put a small piece of felt or a strip of painter's tape under them so they do not scuff the paint. A hanging hamper should hold clothes, not wet towels or heavy jeans by the dozen. If it pulls the door or the rod, empty it more often. If you need extra hooks for the backpack bag or a laundry bag, over-the-door hooks go on the same door without screws.

Two-section hampers and sorters

If you always wash darks and lights separately, sorting on the way in saves time on laundry day. A two-section hamper with a lid does this in a single footprint, and the bags lift out so you can carry one load at a time. A 3-bag rolling sorter adds a third bag for towels or work clothes, but it is wide, so it only works if you have a real corner for it. If you do not sort, skip both and buy one slim hamper.

Step 3: Keep the hamper from smelling

In a small apartment, the hamper is never far from where you sleep or sit, so smell matters. Most hamper smell comes from damp clothes that sit for days. A few habits prevent it:

  • Hang wet towels, gym clothes, and swimsuits to dry before they go in the hamper. A damp towel buried under dry clothes is the most common cause of a sour hamper.
  • Use a hamper with mesh sides or a breathable liner, and do not seal it in a closed cabinet.
  • Wash the hamper liner every few weeks, and let it dry fully before you put it back.
  • Run laundry before the hamper is packed tight. A full, pressed-down hamper does not dry out.

If the closet where the hamper sits already smells musty, deal with the closet first. Our guide to a musty closet smell covers moisture absorbers and when to tell the landlord. And if you dry clothes inside, see how to dry clothes with no dryer so the clean load does not pick up the same smell.

Step 4: Find a safe spot for detergent and supplies

Detergent, stain remover, dryer sheets, and a lint roller add up to a surprising amount of stuff, and in a small apartment it often ends up on the floor next to the toilet. Pick one place and keep it all together so you can grab it on laundry day.

A narrow rolling laundry cart fits in a gap of a few inches beside the washer, the fridge, or a cabinet, and rolls out when you need it. If you already have a cart from the bathroom or kitchen, a slim 3-tier rolling cart works the same way. Put the heaviest bottle on the bottom shelf so the cart does not tip, and keep small things in a bin so they do not fall through.

Laundry pods need extra care. The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission says to store laundry packets in their original containers and out of a child's sight and reach, and not to let children handle them. That means no pods in a cute open jar on a low shelf. If kids visit or you have a curious pet, keep pods in their original container on a high shelf or in a cabinet. In the U.S., Poison Help is 1-800-222-1222 if someone swallows detergent or gets it in their eyes.

Step 5: If you have a washer in your unit

A washer and dryer in a closet or kitchen corner usually come with no shelves at all. You cannot screw a shelf above them in most rentals, but there are two no-drill options.

Magnetic shelves on the side of the machine

Most washers and dryers have steel side panels, so magnetic shelves stick to them without screws or adhesive. They are good for dryer sheets, stain spray, and a small detergent bottle. A few cautions:

  • Check that a magnet sticks to the panel first. Some panels are plastic or have a thick coating.
  • Wipe the panel clean before placing the shelf, and lift it off rather than sliding it, so grit does not scratch the finish.
  • Do not load a magnetic shelf with a heavy detergent jug. Vibration during the spin cycle can shake heavy items loose.
  • Leave enough room for the machine door and any controls to open freely.

A freestanding frame over a side-by-side pair

If the washer and dryer sit side by side, a freestanding over-the-washer shelf straddles both machines and gives you shelves above them, standing on its own legs. Measure the width, the height to the top of a front-loader or the lid of a top-loader, and the depth before you buy. A top-loading washer needs enough clearance above it for the lid to open fully. Keep heavy things on the lowest shelf, keep the frame from touching the machines so vibration does not rattle it, and do not block the dryer vent or the hoses behind the machines.

Step 6: If you carry laundry out

If laundry is in the basement, down the hall, or at a laundromat, the problem is carrying it. A plastic basket is bulky on stairs and does not fit through a crowded door with a bag on your shoulder. A backpack laundry bag holds a full load and leaves your hands free for the detergent and your keys. Between trips it hangs flat on a hook behind a door, which is also where the AskNYC renter kept theirs.

For the clean load, a collapsible laundry basket gives you a stiff basket to fold into and then flattens to slide behind a door or under the bed. That way you do not have to store a full-size basket all week for one hour of use.

Before you leave anything in a shared laundry room, check the lease or the building rules. Many buildings do not allow tenants to store baskets, detergent, or carts in a shared laundry room, and things left there can be thrown away or taken.

Step 7: Give clean clothes a place to land

Clean laundry piles up when there is nowhere to fold it and nowhere to put it away. In a small apartment, fold on the bed right away and put things away before you sit down. If closet space is the real issue, see our guides to clothing storage with no closet and small-apartment storage without a closet. If your problem is that the space feels cluttered no matter what you buy, storage that does not feel cluttered is the better read.

What not to do

  • Do not drill or screw shelves above the washer or on the laundry closet walls without written permission.
  • Do not stick adhesive hooks on the washer, dryer, or the wall right next to them. Heat and vibration make them fail, and they can pull paint. See how adhesive strips peel rental paint.
  • Do not block the dryer vent, the hoses, or the water shut-off valves with carts or shelves. Maintenance needs to reach them.
  • Do not put wet towels in a closed hamper, especially in a closet or a bathroom with no fan.
  • Do not leave laundry pods in an open container where children or pets can reach them.
  • Do not store laundry baskets, carts, or detergent in a shared laundry room unless the building allows it.

Move-out checklist for laundry storage

  • Take down hanging hampers and over-door hooks, and check the top of the door for scuffs.
  • Lift magnetic shelves off the washer and dryer and wipe the panels.
  • Move carts and over-washer frames, then sweep and wipe the floor under and behind them.
  • Empty and wash hamper liners before you pack them.
  • Take dated photos of the laundry area, doors, and closet floor.

Set up laundry storage in a small apartment

Steps

  1. Notice where clothes pile up and measure the space there, including depth.
  2. Pick a hamper shape that fits that space: slim, hanging, or two-section.

  4. Gather detergent and supplies into one cart or bin, with pods up high in their original container.
  5. If you have a washer, test a magnet on its side panel, then add magnetic shelves or a freestanding frame.
  6. If you carry laundry out, hang a backpack bag on an over-door hook and store a collapsible basket flat.
  7. Fold and put away the clean load the same day.

Renter note: Keep everything freestanding, magnetic, or hung over a door, never block the dryer vent or shut-off valves, and do not drill without written permission.

Where should I put a laundry hamper in a small apartment?

Put it where you usually get undressed, such as inside the closet, beside the dresser, or on the back of a door in a hanging bag. Avoid a bathroom with no fan or window, where humidity can make dirty clothes musty.

Is it OK to keep a hamper in the closet?

Yes, if air can move. Use a hamper with mesh sides or a breathable liner, keep wet towels out, and leave the closet door open a little. If the closet already smells musty, fix the moisture first.

What is the best hamper for a small space?

It depends on the space. A slim rolling hamper fits a narrow gap, a hanging hamper bag uses no floor at all, and a two-section hamper saves time if you sort as you go.

How can I add storage above my washer in a rental without drilling?

Use magnetic shelves on the steel side panels for light items, or a freestanding over-the-washer shelf that stands on its own legs. Keep heavy items low and do not block the dryer vent or hoses.

How should I store laundry pods safely?

Keep them in their original container, out of the sight and reach of children, as the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission advises. If someone swallows detergent, call Poison Help at 1-800-222-1222 in the U.S.
