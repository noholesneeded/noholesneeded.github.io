On r/Seattle, a renter in a 495-square-foot apartment described humidity of 65 to 70 percent and mold on a dresser. On r/sanfrancisco, another thread on managing indoor humidity and mold included a complaint that the PG&E bill went “through the roof” while running dehumidifiers. Those two concerns, enough drying and not too much cost, are what this guide is about.

What size dehumidifier do you need?

Dehumidifiers are rated in pints of water removed per day under standard test conditions. Since 2019, US ratings use a newer test, done at lower temperature and humidity. That is why a unit labeled 35 pints today can be similar to an older 50-pint model. Do not compare an old pint number to a new one. For a small apartment, a rough guide:

  • A closet, small bathroom, or a single damp corner: a mini thermo-electric unit, or moisture absorbers like DampRid fragrance-free cups. These remove small amounts of water.
  • A studio or one-bedroom with some damp: a compressor unit around 20 to 22 pints, like the Midea Cube 20-pint or the Midea 22-pint.
  • A damp apartment with readings stuck above 60 percent, or a ground-floor unit: a larger compressor model, often in the 35-pint range.

The square footage on the box is a claim for the best case. A small, very damp apartment can need more drying than a big, dry one.

Measure before you buy

Put a hygrometer in the dampest room for a few days before buying anything. Check it in the morning, after cooking, and after showers. The EPA suggests keeping indoor humidity below 60 percent, and ideally between 30 and 50 percent.

If the readings are high in only one room, like the bathroom, the fix may be the exhaust fan or a door left open, not a big machine. If they are high across the apartment, a compressor dehumidifier is the right tool. A 2-pack lets you compare two rooms, like the bedroom and the bathroom.

Readings near a window or a shower will jump. Place the hygrometer at about chest height, in the middle of the room, away from steam and direct sun.

Mini vs. compressor units

Small thermo-electric dehumidifiers, like the Pro Breeze mini, are quiet and use little power, but they pull a small amount of water each day. They are good for a closet, a small bathroom, or a cabinet. They are not enough for a damp one-bedroom. Compressor units, like the Midea models, remove much more water and are the right choice for a whole small apartment. They are louder and use more power, so put them where the noise does not keep you awake, and let the humidistat turn them off when the room reaches your setting.

What it does to the electric bill

The only way to know your real cost is to measure. Plug the dehumidifier into a Kill A Watt meter for a few days. It shows the kWh used. Then: kWh used × your rate per kWh = cost. For example, if the meter shows 5 kWh over a day and your bill shows 25 cents per kWh, that is about $1.25 a day, or about $37.50 over 30 days if it runs the same every day. Your unit and rate will differ, so do the math with your own numbers.

To keep the cost down:

  • Set the humidistat to around 45 to 50 percent, so the unit cycles off once it gets there instead of running all day.
  • Close the doors and windows in the room it is drying.
  • Clean the filter as the manual says, since a clogged filter makes it work harder.
  • Choose Energy Star when you can. Both Midea models above are listed as Energy Star.

Draining it

Emptying a tank once or twice a day is easy to forget. Many units have a hose port. A dehumidifier drain hose with a 3/4-inch connector can run to a tub, shower, or floor drain for continuous draining. Check that your unit supports it, and that the hose runs downhill the whole way.

What not to do

  • Do not buy the biggest unit by default. Size by your readings and the space.
  • Do not run it in a closed closet with no airflow. Most units need space around the vents.
  • Do not leave the tank full or the bucket dirty. Clean it regularly to avoid smells.
  • Do not paint over or bleach mold on a rental wall without telling the landlord. Report mold in writing.
  • Do not ignore a leak. Ongoing damp from a leak or a broken exhaust fan is a landlord repair.

For the bigger picture on damp, see renter-friendly ways to cut humidity and stop window condensation.

Move-out checklist

  • Empty, clean, and dry the dehumidifier tank before you move it.
  • Check walls and windowsills for mildew, and clean small spots.
  • Look behind and under furniture on outside walls for damp or mold.
  • Report any mold you could not clean to the landlord in writing.
  • Photograph windows, walls, and closets after cleaning.

How-to card

Size and set up a dehumidifier for a small apartment

Measure first, buy the right size, then set it to save power.

  • Time: 3 days of readings, then 20 minutes
  • Cost: Varies
  • Deposit risk: None

What you need

  • A hygrometer
  • A dehumidifier sized to your readings
  • A plug-in power meter
  • A drain hose (optional)

Steps

  1. Put the hygrometer in the dampest room for three days and note morning and evening readings.
  2. If readings are high in one small space, try a mini unit or absorbers; if high across the apartment, choose a compressor unit.
  3. Place the unit away from walls and curtains, with the door and windows closed.
  4. Set the humidistat to about 45 to 50 percent.
  5. Plug it into a power meter for a few days and calculate the cost.
  6. Clean the filter and the tank on the schedule in the manual.

Renter note: Dehumidifiers do not need any holes or hardware. If damp comes from a leak or a broken fan, report it in writing as a repair.

What size dehumidifier do I need for a small apartment?

Often a 20- to 22-pint compressor unit for a studio or one-bedroom with some damp, or a larger unit for a very damp or ground-floor apartment. Base it on your humidity readings.

What humidity should my apartment be?

The EPA suggests keeping indoor humidity below 60 percent, ideally between 30 and 50 percent.

How much does it cost to run a dehumidifier?

It depends on the unit, how long it runs, and your electric rate. Measure the kWh with a plug-in meter and multiply by your rate.

Do mini dehumidifiers work?

For a closet or small bathroom, yes. They remove too little water for a whole damp apartment.

Why do dehumidifier pint ratings look different now?

The US changed the test conditions in 2019, so newer units show lower pint numbers than older units with similar capacity.

Where should I put a dehumidifier in a small apartment?

In the dampest room, or a central spot with doors open between rooms, a few inches from walls and curtains, and where the noise will not keep you awake.
