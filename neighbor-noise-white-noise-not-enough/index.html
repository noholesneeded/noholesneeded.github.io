White Noise Isn't Enough: What Renters Can Actually Do About Neighbor Noise

You bought the white noise machine everyone recommends. It covers the hum. It does not cover the laugh, the TV through the wall, or the chair that scrapes at midnight.

The short version: White noise covers steady sound. It struggles with voices, bass, and sudden bangs. When it is not enough, change the sound (fan-style or brown noise, placed between you and the wall), add earplugs or sleep headphones, seal the gap under the door, and add soft mass on the shared wall. If the noise breaks quiet hours, keep a dated log and take it to management in writing. Renters cannot rebuild a wall, but these steps add up.

A post on r/Apartmentliving titled “VERY thin walls in my new place and I can hear everything” is one of many where white noise alone was not enough. That is not because the machine is broken. Masking works by raising the background sound so other sounds stand out less. It works better on some noises than others, and it only works if you can sleep with it loud enough.

Why white noise isn’t enough for some noise

Think of your neighbor’s noise in three types:

  • Steady sound like a fan, a fridge, or traffic hum. Masking can cover this well.
  • Speech and TV. Voices rise and fall, so they poke out of steady noise. Masking helps some but not all of the time.
  • Impact and bass. Footsteps, slammed doors, and subwoofers travel through the building structure. Masking does little, and nothing you put on your side will stop them fully.

If your problem is mostly the third kind, set your expectations. Earplugs and a log for management will help more than a louder machine. Our guide to quieting thin apartment walls without renovating covers what mass and absorption can and cannot do.

Change the sound, not only the volume

Before you turn the machine all the way up, try these changes:

  1. Try a different sound color. White noise is hissy. Pink and brown noise have more low end, which some people find better for covering voices and a TV. A machine like the LectroFan EVO lets you switch and compare. A fan machine like the Dohm has a softer whoosh.
  2. Move the machine. Put it between your head and the noisy wall or door, not on the far nightstand. Masking works best when it reaches your ears before the noise does.
  3. Set the volume at bedtime, not at the loudest moment. Choose a level you could sleep through every night. If you need it painfully loud, it is time for earplugs or headphones.

Add something at your ears

Foam earplugs are the cheapest strong step. The Mack’s Ultra Soft foam earplugs listing gives a 33 dB NRR. Your real result depends on the fit. Roll them thin, pull your ear up and back, and hold the plug in while it expands. If earplugs hurt or you sleep on your side, sleep headphones in a headband let you play brown noise or a fan track right at your ears, with flat speakers that are easier to lie on.

Many people find a mix works best: earplugs in, with the machine on low. The plugs cut the peaks, and the masking covers what gets through.

Seal the door and soften the room

In many apartments, the loudest path is not the wall. It is the gap under and around the bedroom or entry door. Hallway voices, elevator dings, and a neighbor’s door come in through that gap. A door draft stopper kit or a foam weatherstripping and door sweep kit closes it. Use the slide-on or removable type if you want to keep the door hardware-free. In the room itself, soft and heavy things help a little:

  • A rug and a rug pad on hard floors.
  • Thick curtains, like NICETOWN thermal-lined blackout curtains, on a window that faces a loud street or courtyard.
  • A full bookcase on the shared wall, if it is not blocking a vent or heater.
  • An upholstered headboard instead of a metal one against the wall.

None of these will make the room silent. Together they can take the edge off echo and some voices.

Logging the noise and talking to management

If the noise is during quiet hours or is frequent, a record helps. Keep it simple:

  • The date and time it started and stopped.
  • What you heard, such as music, shouting, a TV, or banging.
  • Whether you contacted the neighbor or management, and what they said.

Talk to the neighbor first if you feel safe doing it. Many people do not know how far their TV carries. If that does not work, send management a short written message with the log attached. Check your lease for a quiet-hours clause and quote it. If you are in a city with noise rules, the local non-emergency line or 311 may also help.

What not to do

  • Do not bang on the wall or play noise back. It makes the conflict worse and can be used against you.
  • Do not glue foam panels to rental walls. They will not block your neighbor, and the adhesive can take paint.
  • Do not run a sound machine at a volume that hurts your ears.
  • Do not block heaters, vents, or outlets with furniture or blankets.
  • Do not sleep in earplugs if you need to hear an alarm and have no backup, like a vibrating alarm or a partner.

Move-out checklist

  • Remove door sweeps and weatherstripping; clean any adhesive residue gently.
  • Check the wall behind the bookcase or headboard for scuffs.
  • Take down curtains and check the brackets or rods you used.
  • Keep a copy of any noise complaints you sent, in case questions come up later.
  • Photograph the door, frame, and walls after cleaning.

How-to card

Build a better masking setup for one bedroom

A step-by-step setup for when a sound machine alone is not enough.

  • Time: 30 minutes
  • Cost: Low
  • Deposit risk: None

What you need

  • A sound machine with fan or brown-noise options
  • Foam earplugs or sleep headphones
  • A door sweep or weatherstripping kit
  • A notebook or notes app

Steps

  1. Note when and where the noise is worst, and what kind it is.
  2. Put the sound machine between your bed and the loudest wall or door.
  3. Try white, pink, and brown noise at bedtime volume, a few nights each.
  4. Fit the door sweep and weatherstripping on the noisiest door.
  5. Add earplugs or sleep headphones on nights when masking is not enough.
  6. Start a noise log if the problem happens during quiet hours.

Renter note: Choose a slide-on door sweep or removable weatherstripping, and test any adhesive on a hidden spot first.

Why doesn’t white noise block my neighbor’s voices?

Voices rise and fall, so they stand out from a steady hiss. Brown or pink noise, a better machine position, and earplugs often help more.

Is brown noise better than white noise for neighbor noise?

Some people find brown noise covers voices and TV better because it has more low-frequency sound. Try both at a volume you can sleep with.

Can a renter soundproof a shared wall?

Not fully. You can add soft mass, seal the door, and mask what is left. Real soundproofing means changing the wall itself, which needs the landlord.

What earplugs are best for neighbor noise?

Soft foam earplugs with a high NRR are a good start. Fit matters more than the number. If they hurt, try sleep headphones with a noise track.

How do I report a noisy neighbor to management?

Keep a dated log, check your lease for quiet hours, and send a short written message with the log. Keep copies.
