When You Can't Control the Heat: Staying Warm in a Cold Rental Bedroom

The short version: When you can’t control the heat, warm yourself before you warm the room. Seal the drafts at the window and under the door, put up thermal curtains or window insulation film, and warm the bed with flannel sheets and a heated mattress pad or blanket with auto-off. A post on r/NYCapartments was titled simply “62 degrees at night is too cold.” In New York City, 62 degrees is the overnight minimum the city’s heat rules require during heat season, so a building can meet the rule and still feel cold to you. That is the situation this guide is for: the heat is legally fine or out of your hands, and you still need to sleep.

Find where the cold comes from

Before you buy anything, find the cold spots. On a cold night:

  • Hold your hand near the window frame, the sash, and the sill. A draft feels like a cold stream.
  • Check the gap under the bedroom door and any door to a hallway.
  • Feel outlets on outside walls. Cold air can come through the box.
  • Put a thermometer by the bed and note the temperature at bedtime and at 3 a.m.

That log helps you choose fixes, and it is also useful if you need to report low heat.

Seal the drafts

Drafts make a room feel colder than the thermometer says. The cheapest fixes:

  • Under the door: a draft stopper kit or a fabric door draft stopper closes the gap without screws.
  • Around the window: a window insulator kit uses double-sided tape and clear film that shrinks tight with a hair dryer. It cuts drafts and adds an air layer. Test the tape on a hidden part of the frame, and peel it slowly in spring.
  • Outlets on outside walls: foam outlet gaskets behind the cover plate, with the power off.

Curtains that hold heat in

At night, glass is a cold surface. Thermal-lined blackout curtains add a layer between the window and the room. Close them at sunset and open them on sunny days to let the sun in. Hang them close to the wall, and let them reach the sill or the floor, so cold air does not spill out the bottom. Our guide to no-drill curtain rods covers ways to hang them without holes. Keep curtains away from radiators and heaters, both for safety and because blocking a radiator traps its heat behind the curtain.

If your radiator sits under the window, tuck the curtain behind the radiator top or shorten it to the sill so the heat goes into the room, not up the glass.

Warm the bed, not the room

Heating a bed takes far less energy than heating a room, and it puts the warmth where you are.

  • Flannel sheets. Cotton flannel feels warm the moment you get in.
  • A heated mattress pad. The Sunbeam heated mattress pad warms the bed from below. Preheat it before bed and turn it down or off once you are in.
  • A heated throw. A Bedsure heated throw works on the bed or the sofa. Use one with auto-off, follow the instructions, and do not fold or tuck it under a mattress unless it is made for that.
  • Layers on top. A wool or down blanket on top of your comforter traps more heat than another sheet underneath.

If you add a space heater

An oil-filled radiator, like the De’Longhi Dragon, heats slowly and quietly and stays warm after it cycles off. Whatever heater you use:

  • Plug it straight into a wall outlet. Never use an extension cord or power strip.
  • Keep about 3 feet clear around it, away from bedding, curtains, and clothes.
  • Choose one with tip-over and overheat shutoff.
  • Do not leave it running while you are out.
  • Check your lease, as some buildings limit space heaters.

A space heater can raise your electric bill, so use it to warm the room before bed, not all night, if you can. Do not put a heater on a smart plug or timer unless its manual says that is OK; many say not to.

Know your heat rights

Many cities and states set minimum indoor temperatures for rentals during heat season. New York City, for example, requires at least 68°F during the day when it is below 55°F outside, and at least 62°F overnight, from October 1 to May 31. Rules vary, so search for your city’s heat requirements. If your apartment is below the local minimum, log the temperatures with dates and times, tell the landlord in writing, and contact your city’s housing agency or 311 if it continues. What not to do

  • Do not use the oven or a gas stove to heat the apartment. It is a carbon monoxide and fire risk.
  • Do not run a space heater on an extension cord or near bedding.
  • Do not block radiators with furniture or curtains.
  • Do not tape plastic over a window you might need as a fire exit.
  • Do not ignore a carbon monoxide alarm. Get out and call for help.

Move-out checklist

  • Peel window film and tape slowly; clean any residue gently.
  • Remove door draft stoppers and check the door bottom for marks.
  • Take out outlet gaskets with the power off.
  • Check walls and floors near heaters for marks.
  • Photograph windows and doors after you finish.

Winterize a rental bedroom in an afternoon

Find the drafts, seal them, then warm the bed.

  • Time: About 2 hours
  • Cost: Low to moderate
  • Deposit risk: Low

What you need

  • A thermometer
  • A door draft stopper
  • A window insulator kit and a hair dryer
  • Thermal curtains
  • Flannel sheets
  • A heated mattress pad or throw

Steps

  1. Find drafts at the window, door, and outlets.
  2. Fit the door draft stopper.
  3. Apply the window insulator film and shrink it tight with a hair dryer.
  4. Hang thermal curtains to the sill or floor, clear of the radiator.
  5. Make the bed with flannel sheets and a heavy top layer.
  6. Add a heated pad or throw with auto-off, and log the room temperature for a week.

Renter note: Test window kit tape on a hidden part of the frame, and keep a written log if the heat stays below your local minimum.

How can I stay warm in a cold apartment without turning up the heat?

Seal drafts, use thermal curtains, and warm the bed with flannel sheets and a heated mattress pad or blanket.

Are window insulation kits renter-friendly?

Yes, they use tape and film that come off. Test the tape on a hidden spot and remove it slowly.

Is a heated mattress pad safe?

When used as directed, with auto-off, and not folded or bunched. Follow the instructions and replace it if the cord is damaged.

What is the safest space heater for a bedroom?

Many people choose oil-filled radiators with tip-over and overheat shutoff. Plug directly into the wall and keep 3 feet clear.

What can I do if my landlord won’t give enough heat?

Check your local heat rules, log the temperatures, tell the landlord in writing, and contact your city housing agency if it continues.

Do thermal curtains really keep a room warmer?

They add a layer between the cold glass and the room, which helps most at night. Close them at sunset, open them on sunny days, and keep them off radiators.
