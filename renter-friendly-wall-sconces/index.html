A common thread on r/Apartmentliving, like this one on drilling and holes, comes down to the same worry: whatever you put up has to come down without a trace. Sconces are harder than art, because a light has weight, a cord, and heat. This guide is about which kind of sconce fits your lease, and which mounting method is least likely to cost you at move-out.

Three kinds of wiring-free sconces

Plug-in sconces are regular wall lights with a cord and a plug. They give the most light and take normal bulbs. You need an outlet within reach, and the cord shows unless you cover it. Rechargeable sconces have a battery built into the lamp. Many sit on a magnetic base that stays on the wall, and the lamp lifts off to charge by USB. No cord on the wall, but you charge them every so often, and the light is usually softer than a plug-in. Puck and stick-on lights are small battery lights for shelves, closets, and under cabinets. They are not true sconces, but they fix many dark-corner problems for very little money. LED puck lights with a remote are a cheap way to try the idea.

Which mounting survives a rental

This is the question that matters most. Here are the options, from most to least secure:

  1. Two small screws, with permission. Most plug-in sconces come with a mounting plate. Two small screw holes are easy to fill with spackle at move-out, and often easier to repair than paint pulled off by adhesive. Ask the landlord in writing and keep the reply.
  2. The sconce’s own adhesive base or pad. Some rechargeable sconces come with adhesive for the base. Follow the instructions exactly and test on a hidden spot first.
  3. Heavy-duty adhesive hooks or strips rated for the weight. Weigh the sconce. Use a strip or Command utility hook rated well above it, and only on smooth, cured paint.
  4. No wall at all. A lamp on a nightstand or a clamp lamp on a headboard or shelf gives similar light with zero wall risk.

Read our guide to what adhesive strips do to rental paint before you stick anything that hangs above a bed.

Never hang a sconce that has a hot incandescent bulb close to an adhesive strip. Use an LED bulb, which runs much cooler, and keep shades away from fabric.

Hide the cord

A plug-in sconce looks built in when the cord runs straight down to the outlet in a cover. A paintable raceway like the D-Line cord cover kit or the slimmer Delamu cord cover sticks to the wall and can be painted to match. The adhesive is the same risk as any other, so test it, and plan to remove it slowly. Other ways to make the cord look intentional:

  • Use a cloth or black cord and run it in a straight line, like a design feature.
  • Put the outlet behind a nightstand so the cord disappears behind it.
  • Use a Kasa smart plug so you can switch the sconce from your phone or on a schedule, and never reach for the cord switch.

Where sconces help most in a small rental

  • Beside the bed: frees up nightstand space and gives reading light. Mount the light a little above your head when sitting up in bed, and to the side.
  • Beside the sofa: adds light at seat height without a floor lamp taking up a corner.
  • Over shelves or art: a small sconce or puck light makes a shelf look styled.
  • In a dark hallway or closet: a puck light or motion light is the simplest fix.

Bulbs and brightness

Plug-in sconces usually take a standard or small-base bulb. Use LED bulbs, which run cool and last longer. Warm white, around 2700K, like the Philips soft white 2700K LED, is a good match for a bedroom or living room. Check the bulb base and the maximum wattage printed on the sconce before you buy. Rechargeable sconces have built-in LEDs. Many have brightness and warm-to-cool settings. If you plan to read by one, check the listing for brightness or try it at night before you mount it.

What not to do

  • Do not cut into a wall or wire a sconce into a junction box without the landlord’s permission and a licensed electrician.
  • Do not hang a sconce on adhesive above a bed if a fall would hurt someone.
  • Do not run cords where they will be pinched behind furniture or stepped on.
  • Do not use a bulb above the wattage printed on the fixture.
  • Do not stick cord covers or bases on wallpaper or fresh paint.

Move-out checklist

  • Take down sconces and bases slowly, stretching any adhesive tab along the wall.
  • Remove cord covers and roll off leftover adhesive.
  • Fill screw holes with spackle if your lease or landlord expects it, and touch up.
  • Check the wall for heat marks or discoloration above the fixture.
  • Photograph each wall after you finish.

How-to card

Install a plug-in sconce in a rental

A plug-in sconce with a neat cord, mounted the way that is easiest to repair later.

  • Time: 45 minutes
  • Cost: Low to moderate
  • Deposit risk: Low with permission

What you need

  • A plug-in sconce
  • An LED bulb
  • A pencil and a level
  • A drill and the screws that came with the sconce (with permission) or a strip rated for the weight
  • A paintable cord cover
  • A smart plug (optional)

Steps

  1. Ask the landlord in writing whether two small screw holes are OK.
  2. Pick the spot near an outlet, and mark the height with a pencil and level.
  3. Mount the sconce plate with the screws, or use a strip rated for the weight on smooth, cured paint.
  4. Hang the sconce, fit an LED bulb, and check that it is level.
  5. Run the cord straight down into a cord cover to the outlet.
  6. Plug it into a smart plug if you want a schedule or phone control.

Renter note: Keep the landlord’s written OK. At move-out, fill the holes or leave them as your lease says.

Can I put wall sconces in a rental?

Yes, if they do not need wiring. Plug-in and rechargeable sconces work in rentals. Mounting often takes two small screws, so ask the landlord first.

Do rechargeable wall sconces give enough light?

They give soft accent light and enough for some reading. For a main light, a plug-in sconce or a lamp is brighter.

Can I hang a sconce with Command strips?

Only if it is light, the strips are rated well above its weight, and the paint is smooth and cured. Test a hidden spot first.

How do I hide a sconce cord?

Use a paintable cord cover running straight down to the outlet, or place furniture to hide the cord.

Are plug-in sconces safe?

They are when you use the right bulb, keep the cord from being pinched, and plug into a working outlet. Follow the wattage limit on the fixture.
