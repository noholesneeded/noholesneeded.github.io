Staying Home After a Canceled Flight? Deposit-Safe Holiday Decorating for Renters

The bag is half packed, the flight is canceled, and the apartment you were about to leave for a week suddenly has to feel like the holidays. You rent, so the usual moves are out: no nails for a wreath, no staples for lights, nothing that peels paint in January. The good news is that a small rental gets most of its holiday feeling from warm light and a few soft things, and nearly all of that can go up tonight and come down clean.

If your weekend trip disappeared, or Friday has you rethinking a holiday flight, you might be spending more of the season in your own apartment than you planned. That is a good reason to make it feel like the holidays now, as long as everything you add can leave with you. Start with light, not stuff

In a small rental, the fastest way to make a room feel like the holidays is to change the light. One overhead fixture makes a room feel like an office. A few warm, low points of light make the same room feel like an evening at home. That is also the cheapest and least risky change, because lights can sit, drape, or clip rather than nail.

  1. Pick warm white. Look for listings that say warm white. Cool white reads blue, and in a small room it can feel like a store display.
  2. Use battery strings where there is no outlet. Battery fairy lights with a remote can go inside a glass jar, along a bookshelf, around a mirror frame, or across a windowsill, with no cord running across the floor.
  3. Use plug-in strings for long runs. Near an outlet, a 33-foot plug-in fairy light string can trace a window or the top of a curtain rod. Check that any lights you buy are listed for indoor use, and keep cords out of walkways.
  4. Put the lights on a schedule. A plug-in mechanical outlet timer turns plug-in strings on at dusk and off at bedtime, so they are not running all night or while you are out.

Hang lights and garland without nails or tape

This is where most deposit damage happens: a staple gun, a roll of packing tape, or a thumbtack in every corner. There are better options, but none of them is magic. Adhesive products can pull loose or lift paint if the wall is dusty, freshly painted, textured, or already flaking. Read the package for weight limits and surface rules, and test one in a hidden spot first.

  • Clips for string lights. Clear mini light clips hold light wire along a window frame, a door frame, or the top of a wall. Clean the spot with rubbing alcohol first, press firmly, and wait the time the package says before you hang the lights.
  • Garland. Command garland hangers are made for wrapping garland along a mantel, shelf edge, or railing. If the garland has lights, a battery-operated pre-lit garland avoids a cord dangling down the wall.
  • Light decor and stockings. Medium clear hooks are less noticeable than white ones. Keep anything you hang well under the rated weight, and do not hang heavy stockings or anything glass over a walkway.
  • Keep spare strips. If you take a hook down and want to rehang it, use replacement strips rather than reusing an old one.

The removal is the part that saves the deposit. Pull the release tab slowly, straight down along the wall, not out toward you. Pulling outward is what takes the paint with it.

If an old strip leaves residue on glass or a door, Goo Gone can help, but test it in a hidden spot first and do not use it on painted drywall without checking the label. Our full guide to keeping adhesive strips from peeling rental paint goes deeper on wall types and removal.

The door, the window, and the tree

The front door

A wreath on the door is the single most "home for the holidays" thing you can do, and it is easy to do without a nail. An over-the-door wreath hanger hooks over the top edge of the door. Close the door slowly the first time to check it still latches, and stick a small felt pad on the inside of the hook if it rubs. For an interior door or a thinner gap, a pair of clear 12-inch over-door hooks is less visible. Some buildings have rules about what goes on the hallway side of a unit door, so check your lease or building rules before you hang anything outside.

The windows

Windows are where a rental can look festive from the street without touching a wall. Static snowflake clings hold to clean glass without glue. Battery LED window candles sit on the sill and turn on at dusk with a light sensor. Neither needs anything stuck to the frame.

The tree

A full-size tree in a studio often ends up blocking a path or sitting against a heater. A 2-foot pre-lit tabletop tree fits on a side table, a dresser, or a bookshelf and packs away in a box. Keep any tree, real or artificial, away from heaters and out of the way of doors. If you want a bigger tree, measure the floor first and leave a clear walkway.

Candles and cozy without the fire risk

Real candles near curtains, garland, and paper decorations are a bad mix in a small apartment, and some leases restrict open flames. Flameless LED pillar candles give the same warm glow and can run on a timer, so they are on when you get home and off when you go to bed. Group three on a tray on the coffee table or a windowsill and you have most of the effect of a candle display with none of the soot.

The rest of cozy is soft things that you already own or can use all winter: a throw on the sofa, an extra pillow, a rug over a cold floor. If the apartment itself runs cold, see our guide to staying warm in a cold rental bedroom and drafty windows in a rental.

What not to do in a rental

  • Do not use nails, tacks, staples, or tape on painted walls or wood trim.
  • Do not hang anything from a sprinkler head, smoke alarm, or light fixture.
  • Do not run cords under rugs or across doorways, and do not daisy-chain power strips.
  • Do not leave plug-in lights on while you are asleep or away. Use a timer.
  • Do not put lights, garland, or paper decorations against a heater or radiator.
  • Do not block the front door, a window you would need in an emergency, or a hallway.

Take-down checklist (so the deposit stays yours)

  • Turn off and unplug every light string before you take it down.
  • Remove clips and hooks by pulling the tab slowly straight down along the wall.
  • Peel window clings and wipe the glass.
  • Lift the wreath hanger off the door and check the top edge for rubbing.
  • Check walls and trim for residue and clean it the way the product label says.
  • Take dated photos of any spot that held a clip or hook.

How-to card

Decorate a rental for the holidays without damage

Warm light first, then the door, the window, and a small tree, all hung with methods that come off clean.

  • Time: About 1 to 2 hours
  • Cost: Low
  • Deposit risk: Low

What you need

  • warm white fairy lights (battery or plug-in)
  • clear removable light clips
  • an over-the-door wreath hanger
  • static window clings
  • flameless candles
  • rubbing alcohol and a cloth
  • an outlet timer (optional)

Steps

  1. Check your lease for rules on decorations, open flames, and the hallway side of your door.
  2. Place battery lights on shelves, in jars, and on windowsills where there is no outlet.
  3. Clean any wall spot with rubbing alcohol before adding a clip, and follow the package wait time.
  4. Clip plug-in lights along a window or door frame and set them on a timer.
  5. Hang the wreath on an over-the-door hanger and check the door still latches.
  6. Add window clings to clean glass and a tabletop tree to a side table.
  7. Use flameless candles anywhere near curtains or garland.

Renter note: Remove adhesive clips by pulling the tab slowly straight down, never outward, and test any remover on a hidden spot first.

How can I decorate my apartment for Christmas without damaging the walls?

Use battery or plug-in lights on shelves and windowsills, removable clips made for light strings on clean, sound paint, an over-the-door wreath hanger, static window clings, and a tabletop tree. Skip nails, tacks, staples, and tape.

Are Command hooks safe for rental walls?

Often, when the wall is clean, painted, and not freshly painted or flaking, and you stay under the weight limit and remove them by pulling the tab straight down. They can still lift weak paint, so test one in a hidden spot first.

How do I hang a wreath on an apartment door without a nail?

Use an over-the-door wreath hanger that hooks over the top edge of the door. Check that the door still latches, add a felt pad if it rubs, and check your building's rules for the hallway side of the door.

Can I use real candles in a rental?

Some leases restrict open flames, and real candles near curtains and garland are a fire risk in a small space. Flameless LED candles with a timer give a similar glow without the flame.
