Fruit flies, drain flies, or fungus gnats? Check before you buy anything

Three small flies show up in apartments, and each one breeds somewhere different. A trap made for one can do almost nothing for the other two, so take thirty seconds to look closely.

  • Fruit flies are about 1/8 inch long, tan in front and dark at the back, and many have red eyes. They hang around fruit, the trash, the sink, and open drinks.
  • Drain flies (also called moth flies) look fuzzy, with wide, rounded wings held like a little roof. They sit on bathroom walls and near the tub or shower drain and flutter more than they fly.
  • Fungus gnats are thin and dark with long legs, a bit like tiny mosquitoes. They walk on the soil of houseplants and fly up when you water.

If you are seeing all three kinds, that is common in older buildings. Deal with the kitchen first, because that is where fruit flies breed fastest.

Quick test: put a ripe banana slice in a jar on the counter and a second jar in the bathroom overnight. Flies on the banana in the kitchen point to fruit flies. Flies only in the bathroom, near the drain, point to drain flies.

Why the flies keep coming back

According to University of Kentucky Extension entomologist Michael Potter, fruit flies lay their eggs on the surface of fermenting food or other moist organic material, and all they need to develop is a moist film of that material. That is why the source is often something you would not call food: the slime inside a drain, a sour sponge, a mop, the bottom of a recycling bin, or a spill of juice under the fridge. The same fact sheet notes that one forgotten potato or onion can breed thousands of flies.

Here is what that means in a small apartment. Your kitchen, trash, and recycling are all in one room, often within a few feet of each other, and there is no garage or back porch to hold bins. The flies do not have far to go. Killing the adults you can see buys you a few days. Removing the film they breed in is what ends it.

Find the source: a 10-minute check

Do this in one pass with a flashlight and a trash bag. Toss anything soft, leaking, or fermenting as you go.

  1. Fruit bowl and counter. Pick up every piece of fruit. Throw out anything soft, split, or leaking, and look at the bowl underneath for sticky residue. Bananas, tomatoes, and onions are the usual suspects.
  2. Trash and recycling. Look under the bag liner, at the bottom of the can. Rinse bottles, cans, and jars before they go in the recycling, because a little wine, beer, soda, or juice is enough to breed flies.
  3. Sink, drain, and disposal. Shine the light down the drain. A brown or gray film on the inside of the pipe is a likely breeding spot. Tape a clear plastic food bag over the drain overnight; if flies are in the bag in the morning, the drain is the source.
  4. Dish rack, sponge, and rags. Smell the sponge and the dish rack tray. If it smells sour, it can feed flies. Wash or replace it, and empty the standing water in the rack tray.
  5. Under and behind appliances. Look under the fridge, the stove, and the toe kick of the cabinets for a dropped grape, an old spill, or a drip tray under the fridge.
  6. Pantry, closet, and cabinet under the sink. Check potatoes, onions, and any bag of produce you forgot. Check under the sink for a slow leak, damp wood, or a trash can that lives there.

Clean the breeding spots so they stay clean

Once you know where the flies are coming from, cleaning the film away is the real fix. Rinsing alone usually does not reach it, because it clings to the inside of pipes and the corners of bins.

The drain and the disposal

Scrub the inside of the drain with a long, stiff pipe brush, pushing down as far as it will go and twisting. Then run hot tap water for a minute. Hot tap water is enough; skip boiling water, which can damage some sink materials and plastic pipes. If a drain keeps breeding flies after a scrub, a product made for the job can help. The Green Gobbler Fruit Fly Killer is a thick gel that the listing says clings to pipes and kills fruit flies, drain flies, and sewer flies. Follow the label, use it at night when the drain will sit unused, and never mix it with bleach or any other drain cleaner.

To stop new scraps from coating the pipe, use a basket strainer. The Hilltop two-pack is a strainer-and-stopper combo that the listing says fits most standard 3-1/2 inch kitchen sink drains; check your drain size before buying. It drops into the existing drain with no tools, so it comes out with you at move-out. Empty it into the trash every night, not into the sink.

Trash, recycling, and food scraps

Take the trash out every night until the flies are gone, even if the bag is not full. Wash the inside of the can with hot, soapy water, dry it, and put a folded paper towel in the bottom to soak up drips. If your can has no lid, that is the next thing to fix. A lidded can such as the iTouchless 13-gallon sensor can closes on its own, and the listing says it has a carbon odor filter. Any can with a lid that seals helps, as long as you clean it.

If you collect scraps for compost or a city food-waste program, keep them in a sealed container, not an open bowl. The Epica countertop compost bin holds about 1.3 gallons, and the listing describes an airtight lid with a replaceable charcoal filter. Empty it every couple of days and rinse it, because a bin that sits for a week becomes the breeding spot. You can also keep scraps in a bag in the freezer until pickup day.

The fruit bowl

For the next two weeks, put ripe fruit in the fridge. Bananas will brown on the outside but stay fine inside. If you like produce out on the counter, store cut or washed fruit in closed containers, like these vented fridge containers with a drain tray, and only leave out what you will eat in a day or two. Cut away any damaged spot on a piece of fruit; the University of Kentucky fact sheet notes the larvae feed near the surface, so the rest of the fruit is fine to eat.

Trap the adults that are left

After the source is gone, the adults already flying around will still lay eggs if they find anything. Traps catch them while the last eggs hatch. Keep traps out for about two weeks, which covers two full life cycles, and put them where you see the most flies, not in a corner where they look tidy.

A trap you can make in two minutes

Pour a few ounces of apple cider vinegar into a jar or a glass and add one drop of dish soap. The soap breaks the surface so the flies sink instead of landing and leaving. Cover the top with plastic wrap held by a rubber band, then poke a few small holes with a toothpick. A paper funnel rolled into the jar works too. A splash of old wine or a piece of very ripe banana in the vinegar can make it more tempting. Make two or three and change them every few days.

Houseplants and fungus gnats

If the flies are mostly around your plants, they are probably fungus gnats, and vinegar will not do much. They breed in soil that stays wet. Let the top inch or two of soil dry out between waterings, empty the saucer under each pot, and stake yellow sticky cards into the soil to catch the adults. The Catchmaster listing describes double-sided cards that work for gnats, white flies, and fruit flies. Keep sticky cards out of reach of pets and away from curtains.

What not to do

  • Do not spray bug spray around food, dishes, or counters. It does not touch the eggs, and it puts insecticide where you cook.
  • Do not mix drain cleaners, bleach, and vinegar. Some combinations release harmful gas.
  • Do not pour boiling water into a plastic drain or a cracked or porcelain sink. Hot tap water and a brush are safer.
  • Do not take apart the sink trap or disposal yourself if your lease says plumbing is maintenance's job. Ask them.
  • Do not stick traps or cards to painted walls or cabinet doors. Set them on the counter or a shelf so nothing pulls off paint.
  • Do not stop after three quiet days. Keep the traps out for about two weeks so the last eggs do not start the cycle again.

When it is your landlord's problem

Most fruit fly problems come from inside the unit, and the cleanup is yours. But some causes belong to the building. Tell your landlord or maintenance in writing if:

  • The sink drains slowly or the disposal does not work, so food sits in the pipe.
  • There is a leak under the sink, behind the fridge, or around the dishwasher that keeps things damp.
  • Flies come up from a floor drain, a tub that is rarely used, or a drain that smells like sewage even after cleaning.
  • The building's trash room or chute is dirty and flies are coming in from the hallway.
  • Window screens are missing or torn, so flies get in from outside.

Keep the request short and specific: what you see, where, what you already cleaned, and the date. A written request also protects you at move-out if a leak caused damage you did not make.

Two-week fruit fly plan

  • Day 1: Do the 10-minute source check and toss anything soft or fermenting.
  • Day 1: Scrub the drain, wash the trash can, and replace the sponge.
  • Day 1: Put two or three traps where the flies gather.
  • Every night: Take out food trash, empty the sink strainer, and wipe the counter.
  • Every few days: Refresh homemade traps and check store-bought ones.
  • Day 14: If you still see new flies, repeat the bag-over-the-drain test and check under appliances again.

Why do I have fruit flies in my apartment if I don't have any fruit out?

Fruit flies breed in any moist film of fermenting material, not just fruit. Check the sink drain, the disposal, the bottom of the trash and recycling, a sour sponge or mop, spills under the fridge, and forgotten potatoes or onions.

How long does it take to get rid of fruit flies?

Once the breeding spot is gone, most people see a big drop in a few days. Keep traps out for about two weeks, because the life cycle from egg to adult takes about a week and eggs laid before the cleanup can still hatch.

Are the flies in my bathroom fruit flies?

If they are fuzzy, have rounded wings, and sit near the shower or sink drain, they are probably drain flies. Scrub the drain with a brush and use a drain product made for flies. If it keeps happening, ask maintenance to check the drain.

Does apple cider vinegar really work as a fruit fly trap?

Yes, a jar of cider vinegar with a drop of dish soap catches adult fruit flies. It will not end the problem on its own, because it does not touch eggs in a drain or trash can, so pair it with cleaning the source.

Should I tell my landlord about fruit flies?

Tell them if the cause is something they maintain, like a slow drain, a broken disposal, a leak, missing window screens, or a dirty trash room. Put the request in writing and include the date.
