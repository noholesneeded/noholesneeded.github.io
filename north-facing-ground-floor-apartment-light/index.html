On r/AskSF, someone asked how hard it is to adjust to living in a dark apartment. The replies split: some people never minded, and some found it wore them down, especially in winter. If you are in the second group, the fix is not one product. It is getting the most out of the window you have and filling in the rest with good light.

What north-facing and ground-floor light is like

In the northern hemisphere, a north-facing window gets little or no direct sun. The light is soft, even, and a little cool, and it stays about the same through the day. That is why artists like north light. The catch is that it is dimmer, and in winter it can feel grey. Ground-floor units add two more problems: buildings, trees, and fences outside block more of the sky, and the street-level view means people can see in, so the blinds stay closed. Closed blinds are often the biggest light loss of all.

Privacy without losing light

The first move is to open the window to the sky without giving up privacy.

  • Sheer curtains. White sheers blur the view from outside while letting light through. Hang them high and wide so they cover the whole frame when closed.
  • Privacy film on the lower half. A one-way privacy film on just the bottom of the window lets the top half show the sky. One-way film works when it is brighter outside than inside. At night, with lamps on, people can see in, so pair it with a curtain.
  • Top-down blinds. If your landlord’s blinds can be lowered from the top, open the top and leave the bottom closed.

Clean the window glass, inside and outside if you can reach it safely. Dirty glass on a north window can cut light more than you would expect.

Fill in with warm, layered light

North light can look cool and blue, so balance it with warm lamps. Use light from three heights:

  • High: a torchiere in a corner, bouncing light off the ceiling.
  • Middle: a table or floor lamp at seat height.
  • Low or accent: a lamp on a shelf or a corner floor lamp behind the sofa.

Use matching warm white bulbs, like the Philips 2700K LED 8-pack, in all of them. A smart bulb that starts brighter and cooler in the morning, then shifts warmer in the evening, suits a room with grey daylight. See how to light a living room with no sunlight for more on layering.

Paint you can’t use, and what to do instead

The usual advice for a dark room is to paint the walls a warm, light color. In a rental, you may not be allowed, or you may have to repaint at move-out. Instead:

  • Use a big mirror. Lean a large floor mirror across from or beside the window to bounce light deeper into the room.
  • Choose pale fabrics. A light rug, a light throw on a dark sofa, and white or cream bedding all reflect light back.
  • Cut the dark mass. Dark, heavy furniture against the window wall soaks up light. Move it to a side wall if you can.
  • Ask about paint anyway. Some landlords allow repainting in an approved color, or will do it themselves. Ask in writing.

Plants that cope with low light

North windows can still grow plants, as long as you pick ones that like indirect light. Common choices include pothos, snake plants, ZZ plants, and some ferns. Keep them close to the window, within a few feet, since light drops quickly with distance. Self-watering planters help avoid overwatering, which is a common problem in dim rooms where soil dries slowly.

Light and mood on grey days

Many people feel more tired or low in dark months. Getting outside in daylight, even on a cloudy morning, helps some people. Others use a bright light panel like the HappyLight Lucent for a short time in the morning. If low mood is affecting your daily life, talk to a doctor before relying on a light box, and follow the device’s instructions.

Ground-floor extras: damp and drafts

Ground-floor and north-facing units can also run damp and cold, since they get less sun to warm and dry them. A hygrometer will tell you if humidity is high. If it is, our guide to what size dehumidifier you need is the next step.

What not to do

  • Do not keep blackout curtains or blinds closed all day for privacy. Use sheers or film instead.
  • Do not paint without written permission.
  • Do not use a light box in place of medical advice if your mood is low.
  • Do not put privacy film on windows if your lease or building rules ban it. Ask first.
  • Do not crowd the window with tall furniture or plants that block the top of the glass.

Move-out checklist

  • Peel off window film slowly and clean the glass.
  • Take down sheers and rods; fill holes only as your lease allows.
  • Put back the landlord’s blinds if you removed them.
  • Check windowsills for water rings from plant pots.
  • Photograph the windows and walls after you finish.

How-to card

Brighten a north-facing room without paint

Keep the window open to the sky, then add light and reflection.

  • Time: About 1.5 hours
  • Cost: Varies
  • Deposit risk: Low

What you need

  • Sheer curtains and a rod
  • Privacy film (optional)
  • A torchiere or corner lamp
  • Warm 2700K LED bulbs
  • A large floor mirror
  • A pale throw or rug

Steps

  1. Clean the window glass.
  2. Replace daytime blinds with sheers, or film the lower half for privacy.
  3. Put a torchiere in the darkest corner.
  4. Add a lamp at seat height and matching warm bulbs everywhere.
  5. Lean a large mirror across from or beside the window.
  6. Swap one dark fabric, like a throw or rug, for a pale one.

Renter note: Use a tension rod or no-drill brackets for sheers if you can’t drill, and test film on a corner of the glass first.

How do I make a north-facing apartment brighter?

Keep the window uncovered with sheers or film for privacy, add warm lamps at several heights, use a large mirror, and choose pale fabrics.

Is a north-facing apartment bad?

Not for everyone. The light is soft and even, but dimmer. Some people find it gloomy in winter, and good lighting helps.

How do I get privacy on a ground floor without blocking light?

Sheer curtains, privacy film on the lower half of the window, or top-down blinds keep people from seeing in while letting light through.

What plants grow in a north-facing window?

Plants that like low or indirect light, such as pothos, snake plants, and ZZ plants. Keep them close to the glass.

Do light therapy lamps help in a dark apartment?

Some people use them in the morning. Talk to a doctor if low mood is affecting your life, and follow the device instructions.

Can I put up privacy film in a rental?

Often yes, if it is a static-cling type that peels off. Check your lease or building rules first, and test a corner of the glass.
