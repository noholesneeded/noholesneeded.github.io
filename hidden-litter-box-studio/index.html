Hidden Litter Box Setups That Don't Take Over a Studio

In a studio, there is no “other room.” The litter box ends up three feet from the bed or next to the fridge, and every guest sees it the second they walk in.

The short version: To hide a litter box in a studio, pick a spot away from the bed and the food, with some airflow, and put the box inside furniture that is big enough for your cat to turn around in. An enclosure cabinet can look like a side table or a bench. A top-entry box cuts down on litter scatter. Use a mat at the entrance, unscented clumping litter, and a sealed pail, and keep scooping daily. Hiding the box does not hide a smell.

On r/Apartmentliving, a post titled “my apartment doesn’t smell like cat, it smells like…” captured the studio problem: in a small space, the box is always nearby. Hiding it helps with looks, but it only works if the box stays clean and your cat keeps using it. This guide covers both.

Pick the spot

In a studio, the best spot is usually:

  • Away from the bed and the kitchen. Cats tend to prefer not to eat near their box, and you probably do not want to sleep near it.
  • Quiet and low-traffic. Not right by the front door or next to a loud appliance.
  • Easy for the cat to reach. Not behind a door that might close or in a spot the cat has to squeeze into.
  • With some air. A closed closet or cupboard holds smell. A corner with some airflow is better.

Common studio spots include a corner of the bathroom (if the door stays open), the end of a hallway, a corner beside the sofa, or under a desk.

Hide it in furniture

A litter enclosure is a cabinet with an opening on one side. The box sits inside, and the top works as a side table or shelf. The barn-door enclosure and the Hzuaneri cabinet are two styles. Before buying, check:

  • Inside size. Measure your current box, including height. Many cats need room to stand and turn. A box that is too small can lead to accidents.
  • The entrance. It should be big enough for your cat, and on a side that is easy to reach.
  • Ventilation. Look for vents or gaps, and leave the cabinet door open a little for the first few weeks.
  • Cleaning access. You should be able to slide the box out to scoop and clean it.

Introduce the enclosure slowly. Some people leave the doors off at first, then add them once the cat is used to it.

Put the enclosure where the box already is, so only one thing changes at a time. Moving the box and hiding it on the same day can confuse some cats.

Cut down on scatter

In a studio, tracked litter ends up everywhere, including the bed. A top-entry box makes the cat jump in from above, which keeps more litter inside. Not every cat likes it, especially older cats or cats with joint problems, so watch how yours uses it. Put a litter mat at the exit to catch litter from paws. Shake or vacuum it daily.

Keep the smell down

Hiding the box can trap smell if you are not careful. The basics still matter:

  • Scoop at least once a day.
  • Use unscented clumping litter, like Dr. Elsey’s Ultra, at about 2 to 3 inches deep.
  • Put used litter in a sealed pail like the Litter Genie, or take it out daily.
  • Clean around the box with an enzyme cleaner like Nature’s Miracle.
  • Run an air purifier like the Levoit Core 300 in the main room.

For more, see how to stop litter box smell in a one-bedroom and why the box still smells after you scoop.

Ideas for common studio layouts

  • Bathroom with space beside the toilet or tub. This is often the best hidden spot. Keep the door propped open with a doorstop so it never closes on your cat, and run the bathroom fan after showers so the litter stays dry.
  • A long entry hallway. An enclosure at the far end of the hallway works as a console table. Keep it clear of the front door so it does not get bumped.
  • An open-plan studio with no hallway. Put the enclosure at the opposite end of the room from the bed, beside the sofa or a bookcase. A low folding screen or a large plant in a heavy pot can soften the view, as long as the cat has a clear path in.
  • A closet with the door removed or always open. Only if there is airflow. A purifier in the main room and a cracked window help. Never shut the cat out of the box.
  • Two cats. The usual advice is one box per cat plus one, which is hard in a studio. Two boxes in two spots is usually better than one large box, even if only one is hidden.

Signs your cat doesn’t like the setup

Watch for these after any change:

  • Peeing or pooping just outside the box or the enclosure.
  • Waiting a long time outside before going in.
  • Going in and out quickly without using it.
  • Using another spot, like a rug or the bathmat.

If you see these, go back a step: take the doors off, move the box back, or try a bigger enclosure. If the problem continues, or your cat strains or cries in the box, call your vet.

What not to do

  • Do not put the box in a closed cupboard with no airflow.
  • Do not squeeze a large cat into a small enclosure.
  • Do not move and hide the box on the same day.
  • Do not put the box next to food and water bowls.
  • Do not use scented sprays or plug-ins near the box to cover smell.

Move-out checklist

  • Clean the floor and wall around the box and enclosure with an enzyme cleaner.
  • Check carpet near the box with a flashlight for stains.
  • Wipe the inside of the enclosure and air it out before moving.
  • Check the floor under the enclosure for scratches or marks.
  • Photograph the area after cleaning.

Set up a hidden litter box in a studio

A hidden box your cat will still use, in one afternoon plus a slow week.

  • Time: About 1 hour
  • Cost: Moderate
  • Deposit risk: Low

What you need

  • An enclosure big enough for your box
  • A litter mat
  • Unscented clumping litter
  • A sealed pail
  • An enzyme cleaner
  • A tape measure

Steps

  1. Measure your box and your cat, and choose an enclosure with room to spare.
  2. Put the enclosure where the box already is.
  3. Put the box inside with the cabinet doors off or open.
  4. Add a mat at the entrance.
  5. Watch for a week, then close the doors if the cat is using it normally.
  6. Keep scooping daily and clean the area weekly.

Renter note: Felt pads under the enclosure protect rental floors, and an enzyme cleaner handles any accidents before they set.

Where should I put a litter box in a studio apartment?

Away from the bed and food, in a quiet spot with some airflow, and easy for your cat to reach. A bathroom corner or a corner of the living area often works.

Do litter box enclosures work?

They can, if the inside is big enough, it has airflow, and you introduce it slowly. Some cats take time to accept them.

Do top-entry litter boxes reduce mess?

They usually reduce scatter, since the cat jumps in from above. They are not a good fit for older cats or cats with joint problems.

Will hiding the litter box make it smell more?

It can if there is no airflow. Keep scooping daily, use unscented litter, and leave some ventilation.

Why won’t my cat use the hidden box?

The enclosure may be too small, too dark, or too new. Take the doors off, give it time, or go back to the old setup. Call your vet if the problem continues.
