Kitchen Storage for Rentals With Almost No Cabinets

In an r/Apartmentliving thread about a small apartment with almost no storage, the kitchen came up again and again: almost no cabinets, and no place to put anything. That is common in older buildings, small studios, and galley kitchens. The good news is that kitchens have more unused space than most rooms. It is just in odd places.

Start with what you cook

Before you buy storage, sort the kitchen into three groups:

  • Daily: the pan, the knife, the cutting board, oil, salt, coffee. These belong within arm’s reach.
  • Weekly: baking dishes, the blender, spare pantry food. These can go higher, lower, or on a cart.
  • Rarely: holiday platters, the extra set of glasses, the bread machine. These can leave the kitchen entirely, to a closet shelf or under a bed.

Only the daily group needs prime space. Many small kitchens feel full because the rarely group is taking up the cabinets.

Use the height you already have

Most cabinet shelves have empty air above the plates. A stackable shelf riser turns one shelf into two: mugs on top, bowls below. They also work on a counter corner for oils and spices.

For cans, a 3-tier stackable can rack keeps them visible instead of stacked three deep. In a deep corner cabinet, a Copco 18-inch lazy Susan brings the back to the front with a spin. Under the sink, pipes are the problem. An expandable under-sink organizer fits around them and holds cleaning supplies, sponges, and trash bags.

Put the heaviest things low and the lightest high. A shelf riser full of heavy plates is harder to reach and more likely to slip.

Add a cart

A rolling cart is a cabinet you can move, and it leaves with you. A kitchen island cart with storage adds counter space and doors. If the microwave is eating your counter, a 3-tier microwave cart gives it a home with storage below. Measure first. You need the cart’s width plus room to open the oven and fridge doors, and enough space to walk past. Lock the wheels when you use it as a work surface.

Use the sides and the backs

The fridge side. If the side of your fridge faces the room, magnetic spice racks hold spices, foil, and wraps. Check that the fridge side is magnetic and not right next to the stove. The back of a door. A pantry door, a closet door, or even the kitchen door can hold an over-the-door pantry organizer. Make sure the door still closes and that the hooks do not scratch the top of the door. Felt pads under the hooks help. Over the sink. A bamboo sink shelf sits behind the faucet for soap and sponges. An over-the-sink dish rack stands over the sink and frees the counter where a drying rack used to be.

Clear the counter

Counter space is the scarcest thing in a small kitchen. Keep only what you use daily on it. Group the rest into one tray or one riser so it reads as a single object instead of ten. For more ways to reduce visual clutter, see storage that doesn’t still feel cluttered.

If you have no upper cabinets at all

Some studio kitchens are a sink, a stove, and a fridge in a row, with nothing on the wall. In that case, think of a freestanding piece as your cabinets:

  • A bookcase or shelving unit near the kitchen. A narrow bookcase with baskets on the lower shelves can hold dry food, small appliances, and dishes. Put heavy things on the bottom shelf so it is stable, and ask before anchoring it to the wall.
  • A cart beside the stove for daily cooking tools. Oil, salt, a crock of utensils, and the pan you use most. Keep it on the side away from the burners.
  • Bins on top of the fridge for things you use rarely, like spare paper towels. Leave space for the fridge to vent if your model vents from the top.
  • Dishes in a drawer unit. A small rolling drawer unit can hold plates and bowls if you lay them flat.

Keep it easy to clean

More storage in a small kitchen means more surfaces to wipe. A few habits keep it from turning into a grease trap:

  • Choose racks and shelves with smooth, wipeable surfaces over the stove area, not open wicker.
  • Keep a little space between shelves and the wall so you can wipe behind them.
  • Store food in lidded containers, especially on open shelves, to keep pests away.
  • Clean the top of the cart and the fridge side every week or two, since cooking grease settles there.

What not to do

  • Do not screw shelves or rails into the wall without permission.
  • Do not put magnetic racks or plastic organizers next to the stove, where heat can warp them.
  • Do not block the oven, fridge, or dishwasher door with a cart.
  • Do not stack heavy pots on a thin wire shelf.
  • Do not store food on the floor. It invites pests and spills.

Move-out checklist

  • Empty and wipe every cabinet, and remove shelf liners you added.
  • Check the top of doors for marks from over-the-door hooks.
  • Wipe the side of the fridge where magnetic racks sat.
  • Check the floor under the cart for scuffs.
  • Photograph cabinets, counters, and the floor after cleaning.

How-to card

Set up a no-cabinet kitchen in an afternoon

A sort, then four places to add storage without drilling.

  • Time: About 2 hours
  • Cost: Varies
  • Deposit risk: None

What you need

  • A tape measure
  • Shelf risers
  • A rolling cart
  • An over-the-door organizer
  • Magnetic racks
  • Felt pads
  • A box for things that leave the kitchen

Steps

  1. Sort everything into daily, weekly, and rarely.
  2. Move the rarely group out of the kitchen.
  3. Add shelf risers inside the cabinets you have.
  4. Measure, then add a rolling cart for pots or small appliances.
  5. Hang an over-the-door organizer for pantry food, with felt pads under the hooks.
  6. Put spices and wraps on magnetic racks on the fridge side.

Renter note: Everything here stands, rolls, or hangs on a door. Check that doors still close and the fridge side is not next to the stove.

How do I store pots and pans with no cabinets?

Use a rolling cart with shelves or doors, the oven drawer, or a sturdy rack on the counter. Hang a few on an over-the-door rack if the door can take the weight.

Where do I keep spices in a tiny kitchen?

Magnetic racks on the fridge side, a riser in a cabinet, or a lazy Susan in a corner.

Can I add shelves in a rental kitchen?

Freestanding shelves, risers, and carts need no drilling. Wall shelves need permission.

How do I make a pantry with no pantry?

Hang an over-the-door organizer on any door, or use a cart or bookcase near the kitchen for dry food.

What goes under a kitchen sink with pipes?

An expandable under-sink organizer that fits around the pipes, holding cleaning supplies and bags.
