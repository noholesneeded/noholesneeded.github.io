Cooling Bedding for Hot Sleepers Who Share a Bed

One of you kicks the covers off at 2 a.m., damp and overheated. The other one is cold and pulls them back. Neither of you sleeps well, and the thermostat is not up for negotiation.

The short version: When one person runs hot in a shared bed, stop sharing one blanket. Use two lighter comforters or a cooling blanket for the hot sleeper, breathable sheets like cotton percale or bamboo-derived rayon, and pillows that do not trap heat. Point a fan at the hot side of the bed. Cooling toppers and “cooling” fabrics help a little, but air moving across the bed and fewer layers on the hot side usually matter more.

In an AskReddit thread for people who share a bed with a partner, overheating came up again and again, often alongside snoring and blanket theft. Hot sleepers are not imagining it. Bodies run at different temperatures, and a shared duvet set for the cold person is too warm for the hot one. The fix is to stop treating the bed as one climate.

Why one of you runs hot

Some people simply run warmer. Others heat up because of the bedding: memory foam that holds heat, polyester sheets that do not breathe, a heavy duvet chosen for the other person, or a small, warm bedroom. A partner’s body heat adds more. Before you buy anything, check the room. If the bedroom is warm at night, start with why your bedroom is hotter than the rest of the apartment and how to cool a rental with bad or no AC.

Two blankets, not one

This is the biggest single change for many couples. Instead of one large comforter, use two smaller ones, each sized for one person, like the Amazon Basics down-alternative comforter in a smaller size, or two very different weights. The hot sleeper can use a light quilt, a sheet, or a cooling blanket. The cold sleeper can have a warm duvet. You both stop waking up when the other moves.

A cool-to-the-touch blanket like the Elegear Arc-Chill or the queen-size inhand cooling blanket uses fabric that feels cool when you first touch it. It will not cool the room, but some hot sleepers like the feel. A twin size on the hot side keeps the cold sleeper’s side warm.

Make the bed in the European style: one fitted sheet, then each person’s own comforter folded on their side. It looks neat and avoids fights over the covers.

Sheets that breathe

Sheets touch your skin all night, so their fabric matters. Many hot sleepers prefer:

  • Cotton percale: a crisp, flat weave that breathes.
  • Linen: loose and airy, and it gets softer with washing.
  • Bamboo-derived rayon or lyocell: smooth and cool to the touch, like the Bedsure bamboo-derived sheets.

Flannel, jersey, and heavy sateen feel warmer. Polyester microfiber is soft but tends to feel warmer and less breathable.

Pillows and toppers

Solid memory foam pillows and toppers can hold heat. Shredded memory foam pillows let air move through the fill, and you can remove fill to make them thinner. Flipping the pillow to the cool side helps too. Cooling mattress toppers claim a lot. Gel or “cooling” foam can feel cool at first and then warm up. If your mattress itself is the problem, a topper made of latex, wool, or a breathable fiber may help more than another layer of memory foam. Try to buy from a store with an easy return policy.

Move air across the bed

Air moving over your skin is the most reliable way to feel cooler. A box fan at the foot of the bed, pointed at the hot sleeper’s side, works for many couples. The cold sleeper can stay under the warmer blanket, out of the breeze. If the fan noise bothers anyone, it doubles as masking sound. See sleeping through a snoring partner for more on sound.

Cool down before you get into bed

What you do in the hour before bed changes how hot the bed feels at midnight:

  • Cool the room ahead of time. Close the curtains on sunny afternoons and run a fan or AC before bed, not only once you are lying down.
  • Take a warm or cool shower. Many people find a warm shower helps, because the body cools down as it dries off. A cool shower feels good straight away. Try both.
  • Wear less, and wear breathable fabric. Light cotton or linen sleepwear, or none. Fleece pajamas on the hot sleeper defeat the rest of the setup.
  • Keep a glass of water by the bed. Waking hot and thirsty is common in summer.
  • Leave the heavy blanket folded at the foot. If the hot sleeper gets cold near dawn, it is there.
  • Wash the sheets often. Clean, dry sheets feel cooler than ones that have soaked up a few nights of sweat.

If the bedroom itself is hot

Bedding can only do so much in a bedroom that is 80 degrees at night. In that case, the fixes are about the room: blocking sun in the day, moving cooler night air in, and cooling one room with a portable AC on a timer if you can. What not to do

  • Do not add another memory foam layer if the bed already sleeps hot.
  • Do not run a fan pointed at the cold sleeper.
  • Do not use a heated mattress pad on both sides if only one person is cold. Use a single-side pad or blanket instead.
  • Do not assume “cooling” on a label means it will feel cooler all night.
  • Do not ignore night sweats that are new or severe. Talk to a doctor.

Move-out checklist

  • Bedding setups do not touch the apartment, so there is little to undo.
  • Check the floor under the fan for scuffs.
  • Wash and pack bedding.
  • Check walls behind the bed for marks.
  • Photograph the bedroom after cleaning.

How-to card

Set up a split-temperature bed

One bed, two climates, set up in half an hour.

  • Time: 30 minutes
  • Cost: Moderate
  • Deposit risk: None

What you need

  • Breathable sheets
  • Two comforters of different weights, or one comforter plus a cooling blanket
  • Shredded foam pillows
  • A box fan

Steps

  1. Put on breathable sheets, like percale, linen, or bamboo-derived rayon.
  2. Give each person their own comforter, light for the hot sleeper and warm for the cold sleeper.
  3. Fold each comforter on its own side of the bed.
  4. Remove some fill from the hot sleeper’s pillow if it is too thick and warm.
  5. Put a box fan at the foot of the bed, pointed at the hot side.
  6. Try it for a week and adjust one thing at a time.

Renter note: Nothing here changes the apartment, and all of it moves with you.

How can a couple sleep with different temperatures?

Use two separate comforters of different weights, breathable sheets, and a fan pointed at the hot sleeper’s side.

What sheets are best for hot sleepers?

Many hot sleepers prefer cotton percale, linen, or bamboo-derived rayon or lyocell. Flannel and heavy sateen feel warmer.

Do cooling blankets work?

They feel cool to the touch at first. They do not cool the room, but some hot sleepers find them more comfortable.

Do cooling mattress toppers work?

Results vary. Gel foam can warm up after a while. If the mattress traps heat, a breathable topper may help more. Check the return policy.

Why does memory foam sleep hot?

Solid foam can trap body heat. Shredded foam or other materials allow more airflow.

What is the best blanket setup for a hot sleeper and a cold sleeper?

Two separate covers: a light quilt or cooling blanket for the hot sleeper and a warmer duvet for the cold sleeper, folded on each side of the bed.
